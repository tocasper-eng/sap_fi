Unit 1: Special General Ledger Transactions

Unit objectives: Understand why certain receivable/payable transactions (down payments, guarantees, value adjustments) are kept apart from normal receivables and payables via alternative reconciliation accounts, learn the three special G/L classes and three special G/L types, run the customer and vendor down payment cycle and an individual value adjustment, configure your own special G/L indicator, and see how down payments flow through MM purchasing and SD sales.

Lesson 1: Managing the Application View for Special General Ledger Transactions

Learn what a special G/L transaction is, how it differs from a normal subledger posting, which classes and types exist, and how to carry out down payments, guarantees and individual value adjustments from the application side (SAP ERP ECC).

Reconciliation accounts: the starting point

Every customer or vendor master record carries a reconciliation account in its company code segment. Whenever an invoice or credit memo is posted to the subledger (AR or AP), the system simultaneously updates the transaction figures of that reconciliation account in the general ledger. This is what lets you build a balance sheet directly from G/L balances without hunting through the subledger. The reconciliation account number is visible on each FI document.

Why "special" G/L transactions exist

Some AR/AP transactions must not be mixed with ordinary receivables and payables for goods and services, either for internal management reasons or for legal reporting (e.g. down payments received are liabilities, not reductions of receivables; guarantees made only belong in the notes). SAP solves this with a special G/L indicator entered on the line item: the posting still goes to the customer/vendor account, but in the G/L it is routed to an alternative reconciliation account (a "special G/L account") instead of the one in the master record. The transaction is therefore shown separately both in the subledger (filterable in line item display) and in the G/L.

Guarantees of payment (type 1 example)

You agree to stand surety for a customer for EUR 10,000. You post a "guarantee made" to the customer with the special G/L indicator; the system books it to the special G/L account "Guarantees of payment made" and automatically to the clearing account for guarantees made. When the liability ends you reverse the statistical posting. Guarantees made appear in the notes to the balance sheet; guarantees received (vendor side, indicator with account type K) are recorded the same way but are not reported. In the training system the guarantee goes to 196100 with automatic offset to 196110.

Down payment request (type 2 example)

A down payment request is a noted item reminding you (or the customer) that a down payment is due. No transaction figures change, so the customer balance is unaffected even though the item appears in the line item list. Customer requests let the payment program collect the amount automatically (requires collection authorization); vendor requests let the payment program pay the down payment. Dunning is also possible.

Down payment received (type 3 example)

The customer pays EUR 11,000 against a request. The incoming payment is recorded on the customer account and on the alternative reconciliation account "Down payments received", with the bank (clearing) account you select as offsetting entry. Because a down payment received before the service is performed is a payable, it must not reduce the receivables reconciliation account; the special G/L account is reported in the payables area. After the invoice is issued the down payment must be transferred (cleared) to the normal reconciliation account.

Customer down payment procedure

  1. Request the down payment (noted item, indicator F or A as target; F-37). No balances change; payment/dunning program can act on it.
  2. Receive the down payment (F-29): shown as a payable on the special G/L account "Down payments received" in the payables area; the receivables reconciliation account is untouched.
  3. Invoice the customer when goods/services are delivered (FB70); the system warns that a down payment exists.
  4. Clear the down payment against the invoice (F-39): the amount leaves the special G/L account and becomes an ordinary payment on the normal reconciliation account.
  5. Clear the remaining payment when the customer settles the balance.

The vendor side mirrors this: down payment request (F-47) → down payment made (F-48) → vendor invoice → down payment clearing (F-54) → final payment.

Individual value adjustment (IVA)

At year-end, disputed or doubtful receivables are adjusted individually. The special G/L procedure fits because the adjustment is recorded on the customer account and on the special G/L account "Individual value adjustments for receivables" at the same time. Steps:

  1. The original receivable is on the customer account (normal reconciliation account, e.g. 140000).
  2. Post the IVA without tax using indicator E and posting key 19 (F-21 transfer without clearing): credit customer with indicator E (lands on 142000), debit the expense account "Expense from individual value adjustment" (210100). The expense is now correctly in the income statement.
  3. After the balance sheet key date the IVA is cleared again, i.e. reversed (FB08).
  4. If the receivable turns out to be uncollectable, the final write-off is posted to "Expenses for uncollectable receivables" against the regular reconciliation account, not via the special G/L account.

Lesson 2: Maintaining Special General Ledger Transactions

Understand the Customizing behind special G/L indicators: the special posting keys, the properties of an indicator, account determination for alternative reconciliation accounts and for automatic offsetting entries, and how to create your own indicator.

Special posting keys

Special G/L transactions are entered with dedicated posting keys that are flagged with the "special G/L" attribute. In the standard system these are:

Posting keyAccount typeDebit/Credit
09Customer (D)Debit special G/L
19Customer (D)Credit special G/L
29Vendor (K)Debit special G/L
39Vendor (K)Credit special G/L

Whenever one of these keys is used, a special G/L indicator must be supplied in the line item; the system needs it to find the alternative account.

Alternative reconciliation accounts (special G/L accounts)

These are ordinary G/L accounts you create in FI and mark as reconciliation accounts for account type D or K. Unlike normal customer/vendor reconciliation accounts, line item display is normally switched on, so you can list the individual special G/L items. The field status group controls the entry screen layout for postings to them.

What you can adapt

Special G/L transactions are delivered preconfigured, but you may change the reconciliation/special G/L account assignments, choose different posting keys or indicators for individual transactions, and change the automatic posting settings (accounts, posting keys, account assignment rules). An indicator is always defined per account type: customer down payments and vendor down payments are configured separately even if they share the letter A.

Account determination

Per chart of accounts, each combination of account type + indicator maps every normal reconciliation account to a special G/L account. At posting time the system takes the reconciliation account from the master record, looks up this table and posts to the special G/L account instead. For statistical indicators a second table (accounts for automatic offsetting entry) stores the fixed clearing account per account type + indicator, e.g. DG (customer guarantee made) → 196110, KG (vendor guarantee received) → 196410 in chart INT.

IMG paths (Financial Accounting (New))

  • Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable → Business Transactions → Postings with Alternative Reconciliation Account → Other Special G/L Transactions → Define Alternative Reconciliation Account for Customers (and for Vendors): indicator properties plus the recon-account/special-G/L-account mapping (FBKP-style maintenance, also OBXR/OBXT).
  • Same node → Define Accounts for Automatic Offsetting Entry: clearing accounts for statistical postings.

Building your own indicator: three patterns

  1. Noted item (e.g. tracking containers lent to customers): create the indicator with the noted-item flag, assign posting keys 09/19, and store an alternative reconciliation account. Nothing else is needed because there is no offsetting entry.
  2. Statistical item with automatic offsetting entry (guarantee pattern): store the alternative reconciliation account AND a clearing account in the automatic-offsetting-entry table.
  3. Transaction with manual offsetting entry (free offsetting): store the alternative reconciliation account only; the user enters the offsetting G/L account during posting.
Key takeaways
  • Posting keys 09/19 (customers) and 29/39 (vendors) are the special G/L keys; an indicator must accompany them.
  • Special G/L accounts are reconciliation accounts for D or K with line item display active.
  • The indicator's properties (noted item, credit-limit relevance, warning, target indicator, class) are defined per account type.
  • Account determination is per chart of accounts: normal reconciliation account + indicator → special G/L account; statistical indicators additionally need a fixed offsetting account.

Lesson 3: Understanding Purchasing (MM Integration)

See how the standard MM three-way match creates FI documents and how, with business function LOG_MMFI_P2P (EhP4/EhP5), down payments can be planned in the purchase order, posted from the Down Payment Monitor and cleared inside Logistics Invoice Verification.

The three-step (three-way match) procedure

  1. Purchase order – created in MM only; no FI posting.
  2. Goods receipt – a material document updates stock; an FI document debits the stock or consumption account and credits the GR/IR clearing account.
  3. Invoice receipt – the vendor invoice in Logistics Invoice Verification creates an FI document debiting GR/IR and crediting the vendor.

Goods receipt and invoice receipt may arrive in either order. GR/IR is managed on an open-item basis so you can check that every invoice has a goods receipt and vice versa. The document types for the FI documents generated by MM are set in configuration.

Down payments in the procurement process (ECC enhancement packages)

Before EhP4 a down payment was a pure FI action that merely referenced the PO number. With business function LOG_MMFI_P2P activated (EhP4; EhP5 adds compatibility with active document splitting), the flow becomes:

  1. Create the PO and record down payment data on the item's Invoice tab: DP category (e.g. V = voluntary down payment), down payment percentage or amount, DP date.
  2. Post the down payment from the Down Payment Monitor (ME2DP): select the PO (red traffic light = open), choose "Down pmnt", which jumps into the vendor down payment posting (F-47/F-48 screen) with indicator A prefilled; a warning about the commitment appears.
  3. Post the goods receipt with reference to the PO (MIGO).
  4. Enter the vendor invoice in Logistics Invoice Verification (MIRO) with PO reference. The system displays a message that down payments on current assets exist; before posting you can press Down Payment Clearing, enter the amount to clear and post invoice and down payment clearing in one step.
Key takeaways
  • Only goods receipt and invoice receipt create FI documents; the PO itself does not.
  • GR/IR is an open-item clearing account linking the two postings.
  • LOG_MMFI_P2P lets the PO carry down payment terms, ME2DP trigger the FI down payment, and MIRO clear it with the invoice.
  • The warning "down payments exist" at invoice entry is the special G/L indicator's "warning on commitments" property in action.

Lesson 4: Understanding Sales and Distribution Integration

Follow the SD order-to-cash cycle and see how a down payment agreement in the sales order's billing plan produces FI noted items and down payments with indicators F and A, up to clearing against the final invoice.

Phases of a sales transaction

  1. Sale – the sales order is created; it is a sales document and triggers no FI posting.
  2. Shipping – a delivery document is created; picking via a transfer order in warehouse management; the goods issue creates an MM material document and an FI document (debit cost of goods sold, credit stock). Billing is only possible after the goods issue.
  3. Billing – the billing document in SD prints the invoice and simultaneously creates the FI document (debit customer, credit revenue).

The document flow shows all documents belonging to one transaction, including their status.

SD/FI down payment process

  1. A down payment agreement is stored in the sales order as a billing plan with a down payment date.
  2. When the billing-plan date arrives, a down payment request is billed with billing type FAZ. The customer receives a letter stating amount and due date; in FI the request is posted automatically as a noted item with special G/L indicator F.
  3. The incoming payment is posted as a down payment with indicator A, selected from the open requests ("Add Down Payment"). It is assigned to the sales order and the request shows as cleared in the SD document flow; the down payment appears among the customer's open items with indicator A.
  4. With partial or final invoices the down payments are carried over as "down payments to be cleared": FI removes them from the special reconciliation account and places them on the normal reconciliation account from the master record, so they reduce the customer's open receivables.

Unit objectives: Distinguish holding a document from parking it, learn how parked documents are created, changed, deleted, posted and reported on, and understand how SAP Business Workflow can route a parked document through an amount-release (dual control) procedure.

Lesson 1: Managing Document Parking (Basics) and Hold Documents

Understand the two ways to save an incomplete FI document without posting it, and why parking is the more powerful option.

The problem

A clerk entering a ten-line document cannot determine the cost center for line nine. The document cannot be posted because it is incomplete, and if the clerk simply leaves the transaction, everything typed so far is lost. Both Hold Document and Park Document let you keep the data entered so far and finish later.

Hold Document

  • Temporarily saves the entry, complete or not.
  • No document number is assigned; the user types a temporary name (e.g. 1000##) when holding and finds the document under that name later (Edit → Select Held Document, or the Templates tree → Held Documents).
  • No transaction figures updated, and the data is not available for any evaluation.
  • Can be completed and posted later, or deleted (Edit → Delete Held Document). Danger: held documents are easily forgotten.

Park Document

  • Also allows incomplete documents; extensive input checks are skipped.
  • A real document number is drawn from the document type's number range exactly as for a posting (externally assigned numbers must be supplied).
  • No transaction figures are updated, but the data is available for real-time evaluations (e.g. amounts from parked documents can be included in the advance return for tax on sales/purchases).
  • Available both in the classic posting transactions and in the Enjoy single-screen transactions (FV70 customer, FV60 vendor, FV50 G/L).
  • Can be completed, changed, posted or deleted later; must be complete before it becomes an FI document; obsolete parked documents should be deleted.
  • Substitutions are not supported for parked documents; they run only when the document is converted into an accounting document.
  • Commonly used to implement the dual-control principle: areas of responsibility are modelled through authorizations (one person enters, another checks and posts).

Lesson 2: Processing Parked Documents

Learn what happens technically when you park, change, post or delete a parked document, which fields can and cannot be changed, and how parked documents appear in reporting.

Parking details

  • An optional completeness check can be run when parking (balance zero? all required fields such as posting key and account filled?).
  • Account assignment templates may be used; posting with reference to another document is not possible for parking.
  • Cross-company-code transactions can be parked: only one parked document is created in the initiating company code; the two real documents with the cross-company number are created at posting.
  • Saving a parked document updates no transaction figures, asset values or control totals; no automatic postings (tax, discounts) are generated and the balance is not checked, although the document overview displays the balance.

Uses and scope

Document parking serves three purposes: temporary storage of input values; multilevel models such as dual control and approval procedures; and work distribution via worklists to speed up processing. It is available for customer invoices/credit memos, vendor invoices/credit memos and G/L account postings.

Changing parked documents

Header and item fields and amounts can be changed as often as needed; the document change rules that protect posted documents do not apply. Changeable: dates, amounts, accounts, account assignment objects. Not changeable: currency, document type, document number, company code. Changes can be made individually (FBV2) or via document/line item lists for several documents, and every change is logged and viewable before or after posting (FBV5, and the "Display of Changed Documents" report with the Parked Documents option).

Posting parked documents

Parked documents are posted individually (FBV0 or from the single-screen transaction) or from a selection list; the list afterwards shows which documents posted successfully and lets you post-process the ones that failed (e.g. missing CO assignment). A batch input session can also be generated. When a parked document is converted:

  1. The normal posting checks run.
  2. A complete FI document is written to the document database.
  3. The change history of the parked document is kept.
  4. The document number stays the same.
  5. Transaction figures are updated.
  6. The parked document data is deleted; the entering user is recorded in the document header.

Deleting parked documents

Deletion is meant for exceptional cases. The number is not lost: displaying the deleted number in FBV3 shows a header with document status Z ("parked document that was deleted") and no line items, so there is no gap in the number range.

Lesson 3: Managing Document Parking and Workflow

Get the fundamentals of SAP Business Workflow (dimensions, architecture, events, tasks, work items, Business Workplace) and see how FI uses workflow variants, release groups and subworkflows to implement amount release for parked documents.

Workflow basics

A business process answers five questions, grouped into four process dimensions: organizational structure (who does the work), process structure (in which order), function (what is done) and information (which data is used). The process is modelled graphically and stored as parameters; at runtime the workflow manager (the SAP system) performs the organizational tasks, sends the right work at the right time to the right person, supports processes spanning several systems, and provides each recipient with the information needed for the next step. SAP delivers workflow templates that can be used, adapted or replaced by your own.

Three-tier architecture

TierSloganContent
Business object levelThe right workTasks are methods of business object types in the Business Object Repository (BOR)
Process levelAt the right timeWorkflow definition = sequence of steps (calls of BOR methods plus control steps such as loops, conditions, container values); maintained in the Workflow Builder
Organizational levelFor the right employeeLink to the org structure; possible processors per task, restricted per step; recipients get a work item in their Business Workplace

Events, tasks, work items

  • A workflow starts with a triggering event, defined per object type in the BOR and raised by the application. Example: the account-assignment-approval workflow (template WS0100000 in the course) starts with the event raised when a parked document is saved as complete.
  • Tasks are the steps of the process; each refers to a BOR object type and method. Even a one-step process can be a workflow definition.
  • Each task has possible processors (groups of employees); in the workflow step you name responsible processors and may exclude users. At runtime the system intersects possible and responsible processors, removes exclusions, and sends the work item to the result.
  • When one processor accepts (reserves) a work item it disappears from the other inboxes; executing it calls the object method. Typical workflow tasks: posting invoices, releasing purchase requisitions, changing material masters, approving leave, creating customers, deleting POs.

For each step, design decides: which object/method (what work), which processors (who, for dialog steps) and the work item text shown in the recipient's inbox.

Business Workplace

Three areas: the selection tree on the left (inbox, outbox with started workflows, documents), the worklist top right (work items grouped under the Workflow folder, with special folders for overdue/missed-deadline items), and the work item preview bottom right where decisions can already be made. Work items are executed by double-click, which starts the application with the data; attachments (SAPscript, Office, web pages) can be viewed and added. Only one user can process a work item at a time; Put back releases a reserved item to status Ready; if explicit confirmation is required, cancelling the end dialog leaves the item as Performed until Set to completed is chosen, and until then it can still be executed or forwarded.

FI configuration for parked-document workflow

  1. Create a workflow variant for document parking in FI Customizing and specify whether document release is active and from which minimum amount.
  2. Assign the variant to company codes (Financial Accounting Global Settings (New) → Global Parameters for Company Code → Enter Global Parameters, field Workflow variant; course uses variant 1000 "IDES FIPP-WF variant"). A company code without a variant releases nothing; one variant may serve many company codes.
  3. Optionally give customers/vendors a release group in the master record so that different partners follow different release procedures. If none is set, the initial (blank) release group is used.
  4. The release approval path is derived from workflow variant + document type + release group.
  5. Release approval path + amount determine the subworkflow and the releasing user(s).

Key takeaways
  • Workflow = four process dimensions, three-tier architecture (BOR objects, process definition, organization), started by events and executed via work items.
  • Recipients = intersection of a task's possible processors and the step's responsible processors minus exclusions; one person reserves a work item, others lose it until it is put back.

Lesson 4: Using Additional Techniques for Substitutions and Validations

Use rules to package reusable Boolean logic and sets to replace long value lists, and combine them in a substitution; note SAP's advice to maintain these objects in development and transport them.

Rules

  • A rule is a named Boolean statement that can be used inside a prerequisite, a check, or another rule. Technically it is just a prerequisite/check stored separately.
  • Use a rule when the logic is complex or needed in several places (e.g. the same prerequisite in a validation and a substitution). Example: RULE1 AND BKPF-BLART <> 'SB' – the system evaluates RULE1 first, then the document type condition.
  • Rule names have at most 11 characters. Rules can also be used in matrix validations at callup point 3.

Sets

  • A set is a flexible, centrally maintained data structure for ordered value lists and hierarchies, originating in FI-SL and used in validations, substitutions, ledger selection, allocations (assessment/distribution), planning, roll-ups and currency conversion.
  • For performance, prefer basic or single-dimension sets over long OR chains or user exits.
  • Syntax: table-field IN set-name, e.g. check that a user in set USER-GROUP posts only to accounts in set ACCOUNT-MANAGEMENT.
  • Multisets combine sets over several fields (dimensions) for cross-validation, e.g. administrative accounts 476510/476520/476530 must be posted with cost centers 1000/1100/1200/1300.
  • Sets are created with GS01 (Special Purpose Ledger → Tools → Set Maintenance): set name, table (BSEG), set type Basic set, field name (HKONT, MATNR), description and value list.

Transport recommendation

Define validations, substitutions and rules only in the development or test system and transport them to production.

Combining rules and sets

  1. Create basic sets for the value lists (accounts, materials).
  2. Create a rule at the relevant callup point whose definition is BSEG-HKONT IN set1 AND BSEG-MATNR IN set2 (sets picked via the Set directory button).
  3. Create the substitution step and use the rule (Rules tab in the formula editor) as prerequisite; enter the constant.
  4. Simulate (choose structure BSEG as data view), activate in OBBH, test with a posting.
Key takeaways
  • Rules = reusable named Boolean logic (max. 11 characters), usable in prerequisites, checks, other rules and matrix validations.
  • Sets = central value lists/hierarchies; field IN set is faster and cleaner than long OR lists; multisets allow multi-dimensional cross checks.
  • Build sets with GS01, rules and substitutions with GGB1, activate with OBBH.
  • Maintain in DEV/QA and transport; do not create these objects directly in production.

Lesson 1: Outlining the Basics and Classifications of Data Archiving

Define data archiving, separate it from reorganization, backup and document storage, and understand the motives, the division of roles and the three-step archiving process.

Definition

Data archiving moves complete data objects consistently out of the SAP database tables into archive files held outside the database (typically on an external storage system). Because a business object is spread over many tables, SAP bundles the logically related tables into an archiving object; the archiving programs then write every table entry belonging to the object together, so nothing is left behind and business consistency is guaranteed. Archived data is compressed, archiving runs online (no system shutdown), and only data from completed business processes may be archived (test data rarely reaches that state).

What archiving is not

TermMeaningRelation to archiving
ReorganizationHistorically (R/2) either physical deletion of application data or DB reorganization (unload/reload to optimise disk layout)Different purpose; archiving keeps data readable
Backup / restoreCopy of the whole database to recover after a failure, taken at fixed intervalsIndependent of archiving; must still run regularly
Document storageElectronic storage of documents (scans etc.) outside SAP with a link in the systemStorage target for archive files, but not archiving itself

Why archive

  • Technical: better and stable response times, less database administration effort (backup, recovery), shorter downtime for upgrades, recoveries and offline backups. Larger databases cost more at every query and every upgrade.
  • Legal and business: country-specific retention rules, access for auditors and tax authorities, possible reuse of data. Archives are hardware- and software-independent so they remain readable after upgrades.

Roles and goals

Archiving is executed by system administrators, not by FI application managers; the FI application manager coordinates with the department and auditors, confirms retention periods, performs FI-specific Customizing and monitors the process. Administrators want the smallest possible database, the FI department wants the most data online: archiving is a negotiated compromise. Long-term goals: keep database volume roughly constant and archive proactively rather than reactively; plan archiving from the start of the implementation because volumes grow quickly after go-live.

The archiving process

  1. Write: the write program reads the data to be archived and creates one or more archive files.
  2. Store: the archive files are placed in an external storage system and administered so they can be found later.
  3. Delete: the deletion program reads the archive file and only then removes the corresponding records from the database.

Writing and deleting alone is not enough; the files must be stored and managed so they remain accessible.

Key takeaways
  • Archiving = consistent removal of complete business objects via archiving objects into readable archive files; runs online.
  • It is neither a backup, nor a reorganization, nor document storage, though archive files are usually kept in a storage system.
  • Motives: performance, administration effort, downtime, and legal retention with auditor access.
  • Three steps: write, store, delete (delete reads the archive first).

Lesson 1: Configuring Document Splitting

Explain the purpose and mechanics of document splitting in New General Ledger Accounting (SAP ERP ECC 6.0), configure the basic settings, analyse postings in the general ledger view and expert mode, and create customer-specific splitting rules.

Motivation

International requirements such as segment reporting need financial statements below company code level. A vendor invoice whose expense lines carry two different segments only has the segment on the expense lines; the vendor (payable) line and the tax line have none. Document splitting projects the G/L characteristics onto every line of the document (in the general ledger view) so that a complete balance sheet per characteristic can be produced. The same applies to Profit Center and Business Area. Document splitting is only needed when you want a balance sheet for a characteristic; if an income statement is enough, do not activate it.

Process steps and supporting mechanisms

  1. Passive split: when clearing (e.g. a payment), the account assignments of the cleared items are copied to the clearing lines.
  2. Active (rule-based) split: lines without assignment (vendor, tax, cash) are split according to the base lines defined in a splitting rule.
  3. Zero-balance clearing lines: if a characteristic does not balance to zero within the document (e.g. a transfer from profit center A to B), the system adds clearing lines on a zero-balance clearing account so that both the document and each dimension balance.

Inheritance and default account assignment (constant) work between steps 2 and 3. SAP Note 1085921 documents splitting with worked examples.

Simulation and expert mode

Since ERP 6.0 you can simulate both the data entry view and the general ledger view before posting (Document → Simulate General Ledger). The Expert Mode button shows the splitting method, the document type used for derivation, business transaction and variant, the item category of each line and which lines served as base for each split. Use it to diagnose errors that would otherwise terminate the posting.

Passive splitting (follow-up processes)

When an invoice is paid (manually or by the payment run), the payment document inherits the invoice's account assignments: the vendor clearing line, the cash discount lines and the bank line are split in the same ratio as the original invoice. The standard rule for payments of vendor invoices is delivered. In the vendor line item display the invoice and payment look as before; only the G/L view shows the split. A residual item created by a partial payment is likewise split passively in the invoice's ratio.

Inheritance

If all account-assigned lines of a document carry the same characteristic values, inheritance copies these values to the remaining lines (bank, tax, petty cash) even without a matching splitting rule, so simple documents balance per characteristic without extra Customizing.

Zero-balance creation and corrections

Reposting an amount from one segment to another produces clearing lines on the zero-balance clearing account. If an invoice was posted and paid with a wrong segment, correct expense and vendor items manually; note that customer/vendor lines cannot receive a profit center by manual entry (it must come from splitting), so vendor-to-vendor or customer-to-customer adjustments are not possible and a clearing account must be used instead.

Typical posting errors

  • No base item category defined for the Cash Account (or VAT) item category in the business transaction/variant assigned to the document type (e.g. SA with 0000/0001).
  • Segment or profit center cannot be inherited because assignments are not unique.

Remedy: either use a document type with a suitable business transaction (an FB50 posting that is really a payment should use a type classified as Payments 1000) or modify/create a rule, which requires a customer-defined splitting method first.

Creating a customer method and rule

  1. Define a new splitting method (Extended Document Splitting → Define Document Splitting Method).
  2. Copy the standard rules with their item categories into it (Define Document Splitting Rule).
  3. Assign the new method (valid for all company codes in the client).
  4. Adjust or add rules/variants as required and classify the relevant document types.

Wizards (business function FIN_GL_CI_2, EhP4)

  • Configure Document Splitting (FAGL_WZ_SPLIT_CONF): guides through classifying G/L accounts and document types, defining characteristics for G/L and CO, zero-balance clearing account, post-capitalization of cash discount to assets, constants, activation and a Customizing check.
  • Create Document Splitting Rule (FAGL_WZ_NEW_RULE): create a business transaction variant, take over assigned item categories, copy an existing rule, edit the rule (items to be split / base item categories), assign a document type, optionally simulate the rule against an already posted document.

The wizards are support tools only; you still need to understand splitting and must test every process using a document type you re-assign.

Course example: customer down payment split across profit centers

A customer down payment (DZ, 1000/0001) of EUR 11,000 with two down payment lines (2,200 to PC 1000 and 8,800 to PC 1402) splits the customer special G/L lines (170000, item category 02100) and tax lines 1:4, but the bank line (04000) is not split and the document cannot be posted, because in the standard rule 02100 is not a base item category for 04000. Solution: copy DZ to a new document type (e.g. AJ), create variant Z0## of business transaction 1000 by copying rule 1000/0001, add base item category 02100 for item category 04000, and assign the new document type to the new variant. The down payment then splits correctly.

Key takeaways
  • Document splitting enriches every G/L-view line with the splitting characteristics so that balance sheets per segment/profit center/business area are possible; activate it only when a balance sheet is required.
  • Three mechanisms: passive split (clearing), active rule-based split (method → business transaction → variant → rule → item categories), zero-balance clearing lines; inheritance and constants fill the gaps.
  • Set Zero balance AND Mandatory field together for a full characteristic balance sheet; activation is client-wide with deactivation per company code; one splitting method per client, always a copy of 0000000012.
  • Simulate the general ledger view and use Expert Mode to see document type, business transaction/variant, item categories and base lines.
  • Customer rules need a customer method; the EhP4 wizards (FIN_GL_CI_2) help but do not replace testing.

Unit 6: New Asset Accounting

Unit objectives: Understand what new Asset Accounting (FI-AA New, ECC EhP7 with New G/L) changes compared with classic FI-AA: equal-ranking depreciation areas assigned to ledger groups, real-time parallel documents via a technical clearing account, the Customizing adjustments it requires, how asset transactions and the Asset Explorer behave; and, finally, what a migration from classic to New General Ledger Accounting involves and how the SAP General Ledger Migration service supports it.

Lesson 1: Explaining the Functionality and Architecture of New Asset Accounting

Describe the improvements new Asset Accounting brings over classic FI-AA and the new posting architecture based on an operational entry document and ledger-group-specific valuation documents.

Why new Asset Accounting

Classic FI-AA was built around depreciation area 01 as the fixed "leading" area and needed delta depreciation areas to represent a parallel valuation under the New G/L ledger approach; APC values of parallel areas were posted periodically. New Asset Accounting removes these constraints.

For each accounting principle exactly one depreciation area posts in real time (exception: investment support areas on the liabilities side, where one of them is designated as the real-time poster).

New posting architecture

  1. The operational entry document (e.g. the vendor invoice part) posts to the technical clearing account for integrated asset acquisition, without a ledger group, so it hits all ledgers. It never updates the asset; the asset master is used only for checks (completeness, posting allowed).
  2. From it the system derives one valuation document per ledger group. Each clears the technical clearing account in its own view (so the account always has zero balance) and posts to the asset reconciliation account, updating the asset subledger with the values of that accounting principle.

Prerequisites for activating new Asset Accounting (figure): New General Ledger Accounting active, the ledger approach or accounts approach for parallel accounting set up consistently, the required business function (FIN_AA_PARALLEL_VAL) switched on, and the Customizing adjustments of Lesson 2 completed; the switch can be set to "in preparation" before full activation.

Key takeaways
  • Depreciation areas are equal; area 01 is no longer forced to be leading and delta areas are obsolete for posting.
  • Every accounting principle is posted in real time with its own document; one real-time area per valuation.
  • Integrated acquisitions use an operational document on the technical clearing account plus LG-specific valuation documents that update the asset.
  • Postings can be restricted to one ledger group or depreciation area with new transactions.

Lesson 3: Using New Asset Accounting

Post and analyse asset transactions in the new architecture: integrated acquisition with activation differences, deactivated depreciation areas, ledger-group-specific retirements, the depreciation run and the Asset Explorer.

Documents created by an integrated acquisition

  • Operational entry document: vendor against technical clearing account, posted with the entered document type, no ledger group; asset data used only for checks.
  • Valuation document parts, one per accounting principle / ledger group (possibly with a different document type such as AA): technical clearing account against asset reconciliation account; the asset subledger is updated.
  • In document display switch valuations with the Ledger Groups button and currency types with the Display Currency button.

Activation differences: if an amount is capitalised under one accounting principle but not under another (e.g. EUR 200 not capitalised under local GAAP), the system creates the operational document, two LG-specific documents and a third document that posts the non-capitalised EUR 200 to expense for the local GAAP ledger.

Further rules: the technical clearing account always balances to zero; accounting-principle-specific documents are always created; a different asset reconciliation account per accounting principle is possible; the technical clearing account is posted even if an accounting principle is not represented on the asset; if no accounting principle is represented by an area posting APC online, an error is raised (changeable to a warning, in which case only statistical areas are updated).

Deactivated depreciation areas

Only the relevant accounting principles are represented on an asset or asset class through their depreciation areas, and FI-AA postings affect only those. P&L postings for the other accounting principles are handled manually. If an accounting principle is not represented by an online APC-posting area, the posting is redirected to the "account for non-operating expense" (T095-KTNAIB).

New document display

All documents produced by one business transaction and valid for one valuation are shown together. Example cross-company acquisition: operational AP document in company code FIAA (no LG), the equivalent document in company code FIA1 (no LG), and the LG-specific valuation document for the asset in FIA1; select "0L + Initial" to see the leading valuation.

Lesson 4: Migrating from Classic General Ledger Accounting to New General Ledger Accounting

Distinguish legacy data transfer from migration, know the questions and sequencing of a New G/L migration project, the phase model with its dates, and the components of the mandatory SAP General Ledger Migration service.

Legacy data transfer vs. migration

A new implementation with subsequent legacy data transfer and a migration from classic to New G/L are both separate projects with full project phases. Neither is a simple table swap: both have technical and process-related dimensions. SAP Note 756146 describes the default configuration and activation of New G/L (new installations have New G/L active by default).

Setting up a migration project

  • Which SAP components are in use (FI-SL, PCA, cost-of-sales ledger, business area balance sheets)?
  • Is parallel accounting (several accounting principles) required?
  • Target configuration: which FI characteristics (segment, profit center, ...) should New G/L carry; period accounting or cost-of-sales accounting for the P&L?

Sequence for existing customers: first the technical upgrade to SAP ERP as its own project; then the New G/L implementation project, of which data migration is one part. Do not combine the migration with a chart-of-accounts conversion or other complex changes. New G/L is activated at client level, so all company codes of the client migrate together.

Phase model

PhaseContent
Phase 0Old fiscal year before the migration date; only balances/open items are carried over, not every document (open items may need BAdI post-processing for document splitting in scenarios 3 and 5)
Phase 1From migration date (start of new fiscal year) until activation: postings continue in classic G/L; documents are checked by the document splitting validation so they can later be transferred to New G/L
Phase 2After activation of New G/L: documents of phase 1 are reposted into New G/L with splitting; productive operation in New G/L

Date example (fiscal year = calendar year, today 23 March 20XX): recommended migration date 1 January 20XX+1, earliest possible 1 January 20XX, sample activation 1 May 20XX+1, document splitting validation active as soon as possible and no later than 1 January 20XX+1. If today is 23 October 20XX the realistic migration date may slip to 1 January 20XX+2 depending on scenario and readiness; 1 January 20XX is only theoretical.

Key takeaways
  • Migration to New G/L is a project in its own right, after the technical upgrade and without other structural changes in parallel; activation is client-wide.
  • Phase model: phase 0 balances only, phase 1 classic postings validated for splitting, phase 2 New G/L live; migration date = start of a fiscal year.
  • The SAP General Ledger Migration service (SV session, Migration Cockpit with Monitor and Process Tree, TV session) is mandatory and secures data consistency.
  • Scenarios 1–5: classic to New G/L; scenarios 6–8: adding functions to an active New G/L.
