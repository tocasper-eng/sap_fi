Unit 1: Automatic Payments

Unit objectives: Understand the end-to-end automatic payment process in SAP S/4HANA: the bank master data it relies on (banks, house banks, house bank accounts, business partner banks), the master-record and document fields that steer it, SEPA direct debit mandates, the full configuration in FBZP (company codes, payment methods, bank selection), and the run itself (parameters, proposal, payment run, payment media, scheduling), plus an outlook on the SAP digital payments add-on.

Lesson 1: Executing an Automatic Payment Run

Get the big picture of what the automatic payment program does and the five processing steps every payment run goes through.

Why a payment program?

Paying invoices one by one is not feasible for a company with thousands of open items. The payment program is SAP's mass-processing tool for payables (and for collecting receivables). It runs in Financial Accounting at intervals the business decides (daily, weekly, ...) and, from a set of open items, decides what is due, how it is to be paid, posts the payment documents, and hands the data to the print/DME/EDI programs that produce the payment media.

From a business point of view, the life cycle of an invoice in the payment process is: invoice is entered, open invoices are analysed for due date, due invoices are prepared for review, payments are approved or rejected, invoices are paid.

Characteristics

  • Designed for both domestic and international payment transactions.
  • Works for vendors and customers, for outgoing and incoming payments (e.g. direct debits).
  • Country-specific flexibility: payment methods, forms and data-carrier formats differ by country and are all configurable.
  • Three things happen automatically: selection of open items to pay/collect, posting of payment documents, and output of payment media (print forms, Data Medium Exchange files, or Electronic Data Interchange).

The five processing steps

  1. Maintain the parameters. The parameters answer: which open items are evaluated, which payment method(s), when payment is made (posting date), which company codes, and when the next run is scheduled (so items that would be overdue by then are included now).
  2. Schedule the proposal run. The system builds a list of business partners and open items that are due, grouped into payments.
  3. Revise the proposal. Clerks review it, drill into each proposed payment, change payment terms/discount, set or remove payment blocks.
  4. Schedule the payment run. After the list is verified (and the debit balance check performed), the payment documents are posted and G/L plus subledger accounts are updated.
  5. Schedule printing. A separate print step produces the payment media (forms, DME files, IDocs).
Key takeaways
  • The payment program is a mass-processing tool for AP and AR, outgoing and incoming payments, domestic and foreign.
  • It automatically selects items, posts payment documents and feeds the payment-media programs.
  • Sequence: parameters, proposal run, proposal editing, payment run, print run.
  • Nothing is posted until the payment run; the proposal is only a simulation that can be edited.

Lesson 2: Using Bank Accounts

Learn the bank master data the payment program needs: the bank directory, house banks, house bank accounts and their link to G/L accounts, and bank details of business partners.

Basic Cash Management capability

Customers without a licence for the full SAP Cash Management can still use a "basic cash management" scope (formerly called Bank Account Management light). Anyone running SAP S/4HANA Finance may use it. It lets a cash manager centrally:

  • Manage banks and house banks (only the subset of fields needed for the payment process).
  • Define bank accounts and house bank accounts (common attributes only).
  • Show bank accounts in a list view, import/export them.
  • Replicate house banks, house bank accounts and bank accounts via IDoc.
  • Monitor cash position and liquidity forecast.
  • Manage memo records (app or FF63 / FF65).

The bank relationship management flow uses "direct revision activation": when a bank account is opened at the external bank, its data is created in S/4HANA in one harmonised step; the bank account master is identified as house bank information and tied to a G/L account in FI.

Manage Banks (bank directory)

Every bank that appears anywhere in the system (your own banks, your customers' and suppliers' banks) has one bank master record in the bank directory. The Fiori app Manage Banks is the central place to display/create/change this data. It stores address, risk and general data, lets you link house banks, contact persons and business partners to a bank, and supports bank hierarchies. A bank master record is uniquely identified by bank country + bank key. Report RFBKABL0 lists changes to bank master data.

House banks

A house bank is a bank at which your own company holds accounts. The house bank record carries the bank master data, data for electronic payment transactions, and the EDI partner profile / DME details. The payment program uses the house bank ID to decide from which bank to pay.

House bank accounts (Manage Bank Accounts app)

Each account is identified by house bank ID + account ID. The bank account master record offers: general data (account number, holder, IBAN, control key, customer-defined attributes), bank relationship data (internal and bank contacts, bank statement data), connectivity to house bank accounts in the central or remote systems, overdraft limits, multilingual descriptions and attachments.

To pay from an account, it must be assigned to a G/L account. Two approaches exist, chosen per house bank account through the master data:

ApproachG/L account typeRelationshipCharacteristics
Balance Sheet Account (classic)Balance Sheet Account1 : 1 between house bank account and G/L account; same currency requiredA new set of bank G/L account + bank clearing accounts is created for every new house bank account; house bank ID and account ID are entered in the G/L account master.

Example from the course: a company paying through several domestic and several foreign banks needs only two sets of accounts — one reconciliation account with one clearing sub-account per payment method for all local house bank accounts, and the same for the foreign ones. No migration is forced: because the approach is set per house bank account, both approaches can run side by side and a company can move gradually.

Business partner bank accounts

When bank details are entered in a BP master record, you only type bank country and bank key; name and address come from the bank directory. If the bank does not exist yet, it can be created on the fly during BP maintenance. Other ways to get bank master data in: import a country's bank directory with program RFBVALL_0, or (lockbox users) a batch-input session that updates customer bank data.

Key takeaways
  • Bank directory entries are client-wide, keyed by bank country + bank key, and are reused in house banks and BP master records.
  • House bank ID + account ID identify a house bank account; the payment program pays from house banks.
  • Classic approach: 1:1 house bank account to balance-sheet G/L account. New approach: n:1 via Cash Account type with subtypes B (reconciliation) and S (sub-account).
  • Basic cash management is available without a Cash Management licence and covers bank/house bank/bank account maintenance plus cash position.

  • Payment methods — the default list of permitted methods; a method entered directly in the invoice item overrides them.

Customer/vendor clearing

If a business partner is both supplier and customer, payment and dunning can net the two accounts. Prerequisites: enter the customer number in the vendor general data and the vendor number in the customer general data, and tick Clearing with vendor / Clearing with customer in the company code data of both — so each company code decides for itself. Partner relationships can be listed with the apps Display Customer List / Display Supplier List or reports RFDKVZ00 / RFKKVZ00 (Account Control and Status selection).

Alternative payer / payee

An alternative payer (customer side) or payee (vendor side) can be maintained at client and at company code level; the company code entry wins. With the Payer/Payee in document indicator, a one-off payer/payee who is not in the system can be typed into the invoice; alternatively, existing accounts can be listed as permitted payers/payees in the master record and picked at document entry. Effect: the open item is cleared on the original account, but the money is collected from / paid to the alternative partner (classic example: payments to an insolvent vendor go to the bankruptcy administrator). Reports: same customer/vendor lists with the Payment Data selection.

Head office / branch

Some customers order locally through branches but pay centrally. Items posted to a branch account are automatically carried to the head office account, which is dunned and paid. If Local processing is set in the head office master, payment and dunning use the branch account instead. The head office of each branch is visible in RFDKVZ00 / RFKKVZ00.

Key takeaways
  • Payment-relevant master data lives at two levels: general (address, bank data, IBAN, mandate) and company code (terms, methods, block).
  • Document entries override master data for payment method, terms, block, partner bank and house bank.
  • Customer/vendor clearing requires cross-references in both general data and the clearing flags in both company code segments.
  • Alternative payer/payee: company code entry beats client entry; head office/branch: items flow to the head office unless local processing is set.

Lesson 4: SEPA Direct Debit

Understand what a SEPA direct debit is, the role of the mandate, and how SAP mandate management supports it.

SEPA Direct Debit (SDD)

SDD is the instrument for collecting euro amounts throughout the SEPA area from accounts that accept collections. Both debtor and creditor need an account at a bank located within SEPA. The creditor initiates the collection via its own bank, based on an agreement with the debtor; the debtor's authorisation for the creditor and the debtor bank is the mandate.

  • Pre-notification: unless a shorter period is agreed, the debtor must be informed at least 14 calendar days before the due date. For recurring debits of a constant amount (rent, insurance premiums), one notification listing all due dates is enough.
  • Goal: fully electronic straight-through processing, including exception handling (rejects, returns, reversals, refunds, refusals, revocations).
  • Between banks, mandate data is exchanged only electronically; between debtor and creditor the mandate can be paper or electronic.

SEPA mandates

The mandate form contains field identifiers followed by space for the data. Identifiers must be in at least one language of the debtor's country of residence; if the creditor cannot determine the language in advance, English identifiers are added. The creditor can personalise the layout. In SAP, mandate management derives most fields from the customer and company code master data, assigns the mandate reference from a mandate-specific number range, and records in the Type of payment field whether the mandate covers a one-off or recurring collection.

Key takeaways
  • SDD collects euro only, within SEPA, and requires a mandate from the debtor.
  • Pre-notification 14 days ahead (one notification suffices for recurring equal amounts).
  • SAP mandate management fills most mandate data automatically and numbers mandates from a dedicated number range.

Configure how the payment program chooses the paying house bank and account: ranking order, available amounts, accounts, value dates and charges, including the differences under the bank reconciliation account approach.

Account determination with the Bank Reconciliation Account approach (self-initiated payments)

When the bank account uses the Cash Account approach, the bank sub-account is no longer taken from the ranking order/accounts folders (IMG: ... Payment Method/Bank Selection for Payment Program → Set Up Bank Determination for Payment Transactions). Instead:

  • Bank determination = ranking order, available amounts, accounts, value date, charges per paying company code.
  • Available Amounts (not Ranking Order) holds the funds per bank.
  • Value date = posting date + days until value date; it drives cash management.
  • Under the reconciliation account approach the clearing account comes from account symbols and masked G/L accounts, as in electronic bank statement customizing.

Lesson 7: Running the Payment Program

Maintain payment run parameters, run and read the payment proposal and exception list, understand payment blocks and edit the proposal.

Payment run parameters

A run is identified by run date + identification (several runs per day are possible). The parameters needed before anything can start:

  • Posting date of the payment documents (defaults to the run date).
  • "Documents entered up to" date (defaults to the run date) — both can be set in the future when payments are not posted daily.
  • Company codes — all must belong to the same country.
  • Payment methods — the order typed is the priority order; the first feasible method is used.
  • Next payment run date — items that would become due (or lose discount) by then are picked up now.
  • Customer/supplier account ranges; optional free selections on document or master fields; optional exchange rate type.

Proposal run

  1. Open items are selected according to the parameters.
  2. Items are grouped into payments; payment method and bank details are assigned.
  3. Items for which no valid method or bank data can be found go to the exception list.
  4. Proposal and exception lists are generated for online review or export.

A vendor item is proposed if, by the next run date (including tolerance days), its discount period will have expired, it would only receive a lower discount, or its net due date will have passed.

The proposal list shows partners and amounts (document numbers and discounts depending on line layout) and allows drill-down to change items. The exception list contains items that matched the parameters but cannot be paid; the additional log explains why.

Payment blocks

Where setEffect / control
Invoice verification (MM)Block can be configured so it can only be lifted within invoice verification.
Master recordBlocks all invoices of the partner; may be configured to require manual removal in the master record.
Document entry (AP invoice)Block reason decides whether it may be removed while editing the proposal.

Additional block reasons can be defined, each with a flag whether it can be changed during payment proposal processing. The debit balance check after the proposal automatically blocks accounts with a debit balance.

Editing the proposal

From the list of proposed payments a clerk can: display the open items in a payment, change payment block and cash discount per item, reassign an item to another existing payment, or create a new payment with a different method/house bank. The saved proposal is then the basis for the actual payment run, in which documents are posted, items cleared and G/L/subledgers updated.

Key takeaways
  • Run date + identification identify a run; posting date and entry-until date default to the run date.
  • Only company codes of one country per run; payment method order = priority.
  • The next run date decides whether an item is paid now to avoid losing discount or becoming overdue.
  • Exception list + additional log explain unpayable items; blocks can originate from MM, master data, documents or the proposal.

Lesson 8: Executing a Payment Run

Understand what the payment run posts, how bank sub-accounts and payment documents work, how the Payment Medium Workbench creates payment media, and how to schedule runs automatically.

The payment run

Until the payment run nothing has been posted; the items in the proposal are locked so they cannot be paid manually or in another run. The payment run then: posts payment documents and clears the open items, posts related items (taxes, discounts, exchange rate differences), identifies payments for EDI, and hands the data to the media creation/print programs.

Country note: where payment documents may only be posted once the bank has settled, the payment method flag Generate payment order only produces a payment order instead; the payment document is created when the order appears on the bank statement, and the items stay blocked for other clearing in the meantime.

Bank sub-accounts

Separate sub-accounts per flow (outgoing checks, outgoing transfers, incoming checks, incoming transfers) hold the payments until the bank actually debits/credits the account (value date); the bank statement (manual or electronic) then moves them to the main bank account. Benefit: the bank G/L account can be reconciled with the bank at any time.

The payment document

  • Document type comes from the country payment method; cross-company-code payments need a second type for clearing postings; both use internal numbering.
  • Header text contains run date and identification (e.g. YYYYXXXX-TA##).
  • Value date = posting date + days to value date (depends on method, bank account, currency, amount limit); a check-cashing time in master data overrides it for checks.

Payment media and the Payment Medium Workbench

The print run transfers forms, advices and payment summary to print administration, DME data to DME administration and creates IDocs for EDI payments. The PMW is the uniform, generic payment medium program: variants per format are set in Customizing, formats can be changed or created (with the DME Engine, without ABAP), all advices come out in one print file with better sorting, the note to payee is freely defined and assigned per origin and method, and mass performance is improved.

PMW process: SAPFPAYM_SCHEDULE performs the pre-service (sorts payments by format, builds payment groups according to granularity, generates notes to payee), then SAPFPAYM creates the media, accompanying sheets, error log and payment summary, and RFFOAVIS_FPAYM creates advice notes and zero-balance notices. Mapping of system data into the format is done by function modules or by the DME Engine, which lets you build bank-specific file formats where no standard exists.

Granularity: the format defines which fields split the output into payment groups (one file per group) — e.g. company code + house bank gives one file per combination. At least one SAPFPAYM variant per payment group is required. Delivered formats' granularity can be refined but not coarsened.

Note to payee: every PMW format has three reference text types — invoice information (classic note), internal reference (for returned media), external reference (for the partner). Contents come from a format-independent, language-specific content template defined in Customizing or via function module.

Automation

The Fiori app Schedule Accounts Payable Jobs with template "Automatic Scheduling of the Payment Program" lets you define scheduling options (recurrence) and the payment run parameters, so runs happen without manual triggering.

Key takeaways
  • The payment run posts, clears and feeds the media programs; items are locked from proposal until posting.
  • Bank sub-accounts bridge the gap between payment posting and bank statement.
  • PMW: SAPFPAYM_SCHEDULE → SAPFPAYM + RFFOAVIS_FPAYM; formats via DME Engine; granularity defines payment groups/files.
  • Payment runs can be scheduled recurrently through Schedule Accounts Payable Jobs.

Lesson 9: Explaining Digital Payments

Describe the SAP digital payments add-on: its purpose as a payment hub to payment service providers, its architecture, and the end-to-end credit card payment scenario.

Purpose

The add-on connects SAP (and non-SAP) consumer applications with external payment service providers (PSPs) for secure end-to-end processing of digital payments, mainly incoming credit card payments. It allows credit card payment methods in S/4HANA for incoming payments, covering tokenised settlement, PSP advice and bank statement automatically. A contract with the chosen PSP is required.

It also handles external payments (online/mobile payment systems rather than cards), either as one-step "direct capture" (charge without prior authorisation) or two-step "charge with authorisation".

Architecture

  • Consumer application (several S/4HANA apps, listed on the SAP Help Portal) sends the request.
  • SAP digital payments add-on on SAP Cloud Platform processes it with a core adapter plus one adapter per PSP, mapping the request into the PSP format.
  • PSP communicates via web services, mostly synchronously.

End-to-end example (S/4HANA Cloud for Customer Payments)

  1. Card registration: the customer logs on and stores a card; later pays open items by card.
  2. Payment run: the AR accountant clears the invoice via automatic or manual credit card payment.
  3. Settlement: settled card payments get status In Transfer; the message goes to the PSP and on to the clearing house, whose reply updates the settlement status automatically.
  4. Advice: the credit card advice records taxes/charges in the cash position as a memo item.
  5. Bank statement: standard FI-AR processing; because statements are highly aggregated, the advice is what gives Finance the detail.
Key takeaways
  • The add-on is a cloud payment hub between S/4HANA consumer apps and PSPs, integrated in SAP's roadmaps (not loosely coupled).
  • Supports card payments and external payments (direct capture or charge with authorisation).
  • Flow: registration → payment → settlement → advice → bank statement.
Practice This unit has no hands-on exercises in the course text; it closes with a learning assessment. Points tested: the payment program posts payment documents automatically; bank master records are client-level; customer/vendor clearing is possible; company code level alternative payer/payee beats client level; SDD is euro-only; payment program configuration covers paying company codes, payment methods per company code and house banks; Available Amounts (not Ranking Order) lists funds per bank; value date = posting date + days; one country per run; reasons for non-payment include debit balance, invalid house bank and amounts below the minimum; the exception list is reviewable before the payment run; the run posts to G/L and subledgers, clears items and feeds print programs; the run ID is in the document header text.

Unit 2: Dunning Program

Unit objectives: Understand how SAP S/4HANA reminds customers (and debit-balance vendors) of overdue items automatically: the four-step dunning process, the master-record and document fields that steer it, the complete configuration of a dunning procedure (levels, charges, minimum amounts, texts, special G/L, environment), and the detailed logic of a dunning run from account selection through proposal editing to printing.

Lesson 1: Executing the Dunning Program

Get an overview of what dunning is for and the four steps of the automatic dunning process.

Why dunning?

When a customer does not pay by the net due date, the company sends reminders: a first, friendly one, followed by increasingly firm notices. S/4HANA automates this. The dunning program looks at customer and vendor accounts whose overdue items produce a debit balance, selects the overdue open items, determines the dunning level of the account and produces a dunning notice. Vendors can be dunned too, typically when a credit memo leaves them with a debit balance. If a partner is both customer and vendor, the two balances can be offset against each other before dunning.

The four steps

  1. Maintain parameters — define how the run behaves; parameters can be copied from an earlier run and only the dates adjusted.
  2. Schedule the dunning run — the program determines accounts and items to dun, dunning levels and all other data, and decides whether a payment reminder or a dunning notice is needed. Everything is stored in one dunning proposal.
  3. Change the dunning proposal — the proposal can be edited, deleted and regenerated as often as necessary.
  4. Start the printout — notices are printed (or e-mailed) and, in the same step, the dunning data in master records and documents is updated.

It is also possible to print directly after the run without touching the proposal.

Key takeaways
  • Dunning = automatic reminders for overdue items on accounts with a debit balance; customers and vendors alike.
  • Parameters → dunning run (proposal) → edit proposal → print and update data.
  • The proposal is editable and repeatable; master data is updated only at printing.

Lesson 4: Running the Dunning Program

Follow a dunning run in detail: parameters, the three internal steps (account selection, line-item dunning, account dunning), due-date and clearing rules, proposal editing, and printing with grouping options.

Parameters

A run is identified by run date (recommended but not required to be the execution date) and identification. The open item selection parameters say which company codes, documents and accounts are scanned. An additional log can be switched on to trace the run, but only for test/training because it is resource-hungry. Parameters can be copied from earlier runs.

The dunning run

The run produces a proposal that can be edited, deleted and recreated. Printing can be triggered immediately after the run, at the cost of not being able to edit. Internally there are three steps:

  1. Account selection. An account qualifies if it has a dunning procedure and its last-dunned date plus the dunning interval has been reached.
  2. Dun line items. For each selected account, overdue items are identified and given a dunning level.
  3. Dun account. The program decides whether the account must be dunned at all and at which level.

Due dates

ItemDue date for dunning
ReceivableNet due date.
Invoice-related credit memoSame as the invoice; its own payment terms are ignored unless V is entered in the invoice reference field.
Other credit memoBaseline date.

Clearing with credit memos and vendor items

Due credit items are netted against due debit items, starting with the debit items of the highest dunning level. If customer/vendor clearing is active (same dunning procedure on both), due vendor credits are netted as well. After netting, the account must still have a debit balance to be dunned — unless the procedure flag "dun even if balance positive" is set. Each notice must have a debit balance; cleared items are listed on the notice.

Dunning date, grace period, blocks and payment methods

  • Due date = day the money was owed; dunning date = day the item is dunned. Every dunned item is overdue, but not every overdue item is dunned — the line-item grace period excludes items that only just became overdue.
  • Items with a dunning block go to the blocked items list; accounts with a block go to the blocked accounts list.
  • Items or accounts with an incoming payment method are skipped (the payment program collects them) unless they carry a payment block.

Determining dunning levels

  • An item's level follows its days in arrears according to the procedure (max. nine levels); the higher the level, the sterner the text.
  • Invoice-related credit memos inherit the invoice's level. A level can increase only by one per run — no skipping.
  • Procedures with a single level are payment reminders, used for key accounts and public bodies.
  • Dunning keys cap the level an item may reach.
  • Minimum amount/percentage per level: if the total of items at a level is too small, they fall to a lower level (higher levels normally have higher minimums).
  • Minimum days in arrears (account): at least one item must reach it. Course example: items 1, 3 and 4 are overdue beyond the grace period, item 2 is within the grace period; because item 4 reaches the account minimum, items 1, 3 and 4 are dunned and item 2 is not.
  • The account receives the highest level of its dunned items and the text is chosen accordingly. Levels are only written to items and accounts at print time, although they are already determined in the run.

Is a notice necessary?

An account is dunned only if the dunning data changed since the last run or the level has the Always dun flag (normal for the last level and for payment reminders). Accounts with a legal dunning procedure start date in the master record follow a different rule: they are dunned if postings occurred since the last run or Always dun in legal dunning procedure is set; the output is an internal note on a special form for the legal department rather than a letter to the customer, and the flag makes sure items posted before the legal procedure started are not overlooked.

Editing the proposal

Supporting lists: dunning statistics, dunning list, blocked accounts, blocked line items, dunning history; a sample printout can be previewed. The clerk can block/unblock accounts and items in the proposal and lower an item's level. Changes to master data or documents do not affect the current run — the proposal must be deleted and recreated. A proposal that will not be printed must be deleted, otherwise its items stay locked for other dunning runs.

Printing

The print program groups items into notices, generates one notice per group, and writes dunning date and level into items and accounts, printing in the order given by sort criteria. For one-time customers only the item data is updated. SAP Note 6591 describes how to undo dunning data changes.

Grouping rules: items go on the same notice when company code, dunning area and account match (one-time accounts: same address). Special groupings:

  • Dunning by level — company-code setting for one notice per level, text chosen per level.
  • Grouping key — in the master record; groups items with equal values in fields of BSID/BSIK.
  • Local processing — head office normally gets one notice for all branches; if set in the branch accounts, branches are dunned locally.
  • Cross-company-code dunning — assign company codes to a dunning company code; one notice combines a customer's items from all of them using the procedure in the dunning company code, while interval and blocks are checked per company code. A legal dunning date in the dunning company code also applies to dependent codes.

Payment forms and advices: a bank transfer form (Germany) or ESR form (Switzerland) can be attached or printed on a separate page (requires a form window or separate form); the notice may contain only company code currency items. The payment program can create a payment advice (type 08, 10-digit number starting with 08, type must be configured) listing the dunned items so the incoming payment can be assigned; its number can be printed on notice and form.

Key takeaways
  • Three run steps: account selection (procedure + interval), line-item dunning (level by arrears), account dunning (debit balance, minimum days, changed data or Always dun).
  • Reasons for not dunning overdue items: not in parameters, within grace period, dunning block, incoming payment method without block, below minimum amount, netted by credit items, account minimum days not reached.
  • Credit memos: invoice-related = invoice due date (unless V), others = baseline date.
  • Only changes made inside the proposal count for the current run; printing updates master records and documents.
  • Grouping: company code + dunning area + account, with options per level, grouping key, local processing and cross-company-code dunning.

Lesson 1: Explaining Correspondence

Define correspondence and explain why a company automates it.

What correspondence is

Correspondence is any formal communication between the company and a customer or vendor that relates to the financial transactions recorded in the system. The correspondence function can automate most periodic mailings, run on demand, or be triggered automatically after specific transactions (for example a payment with a difference). Typical outputs: account statements, open item lists, individual letters, standard letters, balance confirmations and document extracts. Output can be printed or sent electronically (e-mail).

Why it matters

Correspondence formally performs and documents communication with third parties. By sending a partner your view of the account, you verify that both sides have a consistent picture of all transactions, so errors and misunderstandings surface early and can be corrected proactively.

Key takeaways
  • Correspondence = formal, documented communication about FI transactions with business partners.
  • It can be periodic, on demand, or event-driven; printed or e-mailed.
  • Purpose: reconcile the company's view of the account with the partner's and catch discrepancies early.

Lesson 1: Managing the Application View for Special G/L Transactions

Explain the concept of special G/L transactions, their classes and types, and work through the business processes for down payments, individual value adjustments and guarantees of payment.

Reconciliation accounts

Every customer or vendor master record holds a reconciliation account in its company code segment. Each subledger posting (invoice, credit memo, payment) is simultaneously posted to this G/L account, so totals for receivables and payables are available in the general ledger at any time and financial statements can be produced without reading the subledger. The reconciliation account number appears in the FI document, and its transaction figures are updated with each posting.

Alternative reconciliation accounts

Some AR/AP transactions must be shown separately in the G/L and subledger — for internal reasons or for reporting rules; for instance, down payments may not be shown in the balance sheet together with trade receivables/payables. When a line item is posted with a special G/L indicator, the system does not use the reconciliation account from the master record but an alternative reconciliation account (special G/L account) assigned in Customizing. These postings are called special G/L transactions.

The liability appears on the customer account and on the special G/L account "guarantees of payment made", with the automatic offsetting entry on the clearing account for guarantees made. When the liability ends, the guarantee is cancelled (reversed). Guarantees made are reported in the notes; guarantees received are not displayed.

After invoicing, the down payment is transferred/cleared.

Down payment procedures

Customer side:

  1. Down payment request (noted item; no balance change; can be dunned and collected automatically — collection authorisation needed).
  2. Down payment received (shown as a payable on the "down payments received" alternative reconciliation account).
  3. Customer invoice for the goods/services.
  4. Down payment cleared against the invoice — it ceases to be a down payment and becomes a payment on the normal reconciliation account.
  5. Remaining balance is paid and cleared.

Vendor side: post the down payment request, enter the down payment made, receive the vendor invoice, clear the down payment, clear the final payment. Vendor down payment requests exist so that the payment program can execute down payments.

Individual value adjustment

At year-end, disputed or doubtful receivables are recorded as individual value adjustments. Because the entry hits both the customer account and the special G/L account "individual value adjustments for receivables", the special G/L procedure applies:

  1. The receivable exists on the customer account.
  2. The adjustment (without tax) is posted; the expense lands on "expense for individual value adjustment" for the income statement.
  3. After the balance sheet key date the adjustment is cleared (reversed).
  4. If the receivable turns out to be uncollectable, the final write-off is posted to "expenses for uncollectable receivables" — on the regular reconciliation account, not the special G/L account.

Guarantees of payment

Customer: a guarantee made (EUR 10,000) is posted on the customer and automatically on the special G/L account, offset against the clearing account "guarantee made"; posting (1) creates, posting (2) cancels. Vendor: a guarantee received from a vendor is shown on the vendor account and a special G/L account in the same way. Both are stored as transactions with automatic offsetting entries.

Key takeaways
  • Special G/L indicator → alternative reconciliation account → separate display in G/L and subledger.
  • Classes = business purpose (down payments, bills of exchange, other); types = posting technique (statistical automatic offsetting, noted item, free offsetting).
  • Noted items update no transaction figures and have no offsetting entry; they drive payment and dunning.
  • Customer down payments received are payables and must not touch the receivables reconciliation account until cleared with the invoice.
  • Doubtful receivables are individual (not flat-rate) value adjustments; final write-offs use the normal reconciliation account.

Lesson 3: Understanding Purchasing (MM Integration)

Understand the three-way match in procurement and how down payments linked to purchase orders are posted and cleared in Logistics Invoice Verification.

Three-step reconciliation (three-way match)

  1. Purchase order — created only in MM; no FI posting.
  2. Goods receipt — material document in MM updates stock; an FI document debits the stock account (or consumption account for consumables) and credits the GR/IR clearing account.
  3. Invoice receipt — the vendor invoice posted in MM creates an FI document: debit GR/IR, credit vendor.

Goods receipt and invoice receipt may arrive in either order. The GR/IR account is managed on an open item basis so that every invoice can be matched to a receipt and vice versa. FI document types for these postings are set in configuration.

Down payments with purchase orders

Business function LOG_MMFI_P2P must be active. Since EhP4 down payments/requests can be controlled from the purchase order and cleared in the vendor invoice; since EhP5 this also works with active document splitting. Process:

  1. Create the purchase order and enter the down payment information (the vendor wants a down payment).
  2. Post the down payment via the Down Payment Monitor (ME2DP).
  3. Post the goods receipt with reference to the PO as usual.
  4. Post the invoice in Logistics Invoice Verification with PO reference; the system warns that a down payment exists for this order and allows clearing the down payment inside the invoice transaction.
Key takeaways
  • PO: MM only. GR: stock/consumption vs GR/IR. IR: GR/IR vs vendor.
  • GR/IR is an open-item-managed clearing account that ties receipts to invoices.
  • With LOG_MMFI_P2P, down payments are planned in the PO, posted with ME2DP and cleared during invoice verification.

Lesson 4: Understanding Sales and Distribution Integration

Follow the sales process from order to billing and see how down payment requests and down payments agreed in a sales order flow into FI as special G/L transactions.

Phases of a sales transaction

  1. Sale — the sales order is created; it is an SD document with no FI posting.
  2. Shipping — a delivery document is created; picking via a transfer order in warehouse management; the goods issue creates an MM material document and an FI document (debit cost of goods sold, credit stock). Billing is possible only after the goods issue.
  3. Billing — the billing document is created in SD, the customer gets the invoice, and an FI document posts debit customer / credit revenue.

The document flow shows all documents belonging to one transaction.

Down payments in SD

  • A down payment agreement is entered in the sales order as a billing plan. When the billing date is reached, a down payment request is created with billing type FAZ, a letter with amount and due date goes to the customer, and FI receives a noted item with special G/L indicator F.
  • When the customer pays, the incoming payment is assigned to the request (select the open request, "Add Down Payment") and posted with special G/L indicator A; it appears among the AR open items and the request shows as cleared in the SD document flow.
  • With partial or final invoices, the down payments are transferred as "down payments to be cleared": they leave the special reconciliation account and move to the customer's normal reconciliation account, so they appear among the customer's open items and reduce total receivables.
Key takeaways
  • Sales order: no FI posting; goods issue: COGS vs stock; billing: customer vs revenue.

Lesson 1: Managing Document Parking Basics and Document Holding

Distinguish holding a document from parking a document and know when each is appropriate.

The problem

An accountant is keying a ten-line document and cannot determine the cost centre for line nine. The document is incomplete, so it cannot be posted; if entry is abandoned, everything typed so far is lost. The department needs a way to save the data entered so far and resume later. SAP offers two mechanisms: Hold Document and Park Document.

Holding documents

  • Temporarily saves the data entered; the document may be incomplete.
  • No document number is assigned — the user gives the held document a name and retrieves it under that name.
  • No account balances are updated and the data is not available for any evaluation.
  • Later the user can complete, post or delete it. Risk: held documents are easily forgotten because they are invisible to everyone else.

Parking documents

  • Also saves incomplete documents without the full input checks, but the system assigns a real document number from the document type's number range (an externally assigned number must be supplied).
  • Transaction figures are not updated, yet the data is available for real-time evaluations — e.g. amounts in parked documents can be included in the advance return for tax on sales/purchases.
  • Available for classic posting transactions and for the new posting screen.
  • Parked documents can be completed, changed, posted or deleted later; they must be complete before they can become proper FI documents; unneeded ones should be deleted.
  • Substitutions are not supported for parked documents — they apply only once the document is turned into an accounting document.
  • Parking is the more powerful tool and is widely used to implement the dual-control principle (one person enters, another checks and posts), with authorisations modelling areas of responsibility.

Lesson 2: Processing Parked Documents

Create, change, post and delete parked documents, and understand what changes when a parked document becomes a posted one.

Creating parked documents

  • Possible for customer accounts (invoices, credit memos), vendor accounts (invoices, credit memos) and G/L account postings.
  • Account assignment templates can be used; reference documents (post with reference) cannot.
  • Cross-company-code transactions can be parked: only one parked document is created in the initiating company code; on posting, two documents linked by a cross-company-code number are created.
  • On saving: no transaction figures, asset values or control totals are updated, no automatic postings are generated, and the balance is not checked — although the document overview shows the current balance.

Uses: temporary storage of input, multi-level models (dual control, approval procedures), and work distribution through worklists to speed up processing. The usual path is edit → post; deletion is the exception.

Editing

Header and line item fields and amounts can be changed as often as needed — dates, amounts, accounts, account assignment objects. The document change rules that restrict posted documents do not apply. Fields that cannot be changed: currency, document type, document number, company code.

Posting

Parked documents are posted individually or via a selection list; after a mass post the system lists which ones succeeded, and those that failed (e.g. missing cost assignment) can be post-processed from the list. A batch input session can also be generated. On conversion to a regular document:

  1. All normal posting checks run.
  2. A complete FI document is written to the document database.
  3. The change history of the parked document is kept.
  4. The document number is retained — the parked number becomes the posted number (a deleted parked document's number cannot be reused).
  5. Transaction figures are updated and the parked document is deleted.
  6. The original entering user is recorded in the document header.

Reporting

Parked documents are integrated into account display and FI reporting: many reports can include them, they can be shown in the document journal and in the line item display.

Lesson 2: Maintaining and Executing Validations

Why custom validations

Most input is already checked by programs, tables and master data, but company-specific rules (only certain accounts for closing entries, only certain cost centres for certain expenses) cannot be standardised. Validations give G/L, AR and AP owners extra control over data consistency.

Step structure

  1. Prerequisite — if true, the check is executed; if false, the step is skipped.

Lesson 4: Using Additional Techniques for Substitutions and Validations

Use rules to encapsulate reusable logic and sets (including multisets) to replace long boolean lists and perform cross-validations.

Transport recommendation

Validations, substitutions and rules should be created only in the development or test system and transported to production.

Rules

A rule is a named boolean statement (name up to 11 characters) that can be used inside a prerequisite, a check or another rule. Technically it is nothing more than a stored prerequisite/check, but it lets you summarise complex logic and reuse it — e.g. a long prerequisite needed both in a validation and in a substitution. Example: RULE1 AND BKPF-BLART <> 'SB' — the system evaluates RULE1 first, then the document type condition. Rules may also appear in statements with arithmetic processing and in matrix validations at callup point 3.

Sets

A set is a flexible, centrally maintained data structure holding ordered values or hierarchies; it is used throughout FI-SL — in validation formulas, substitutions and ledger selection, allocations (assessment/distribution), planning, roll-ups and currency conversion. For performance, prefer basic or single-dimension sets over long boolean lists or user exits. Syntax: table-field IN setname.

  • Example 1: if the user is in set USER-GROUP, check that the account is in set ACCOUNT-MANAGEMENT.
  • Example 2 (multiset): a multiset combines sets for several fields (dimensions) to cross-validate characteristics. If one of the expense accounts 61003000, 61004000, 61005000 or 61006000 is entered, the cost centre must be the corresponding one (TA##1100, TA##1200, TA##1300 or TA##1400).
Key takeaways
  • Rules = reusable named boolean statements (11 characters), evaluated before the rest of the expression.
  • Sets replace long value lists; syntax field IN set; basic/single-dimension sets perform best.
  • Multisets allow cross-validation of combinations such as account × cost centre.
  • Develop and test in DEV/QA, transport to production.

Unit 7: Data Archiving in FI

Lesson 1: Outlining the Basics and Classifications of Data Archiving

Define data archiving, explain the reasons and legal constraints behind it, the roles involved, and the generic three-step process.

Why archive?

Large database volumes slow down queries for users and increase administration effort. Data that is no longer needed in daily operations should therefore leave the database — but it cannot simply be deleted, because read access must remain possible (auditors, tax authorities, complaints). Archiving moves the data to external storage in a hardware- and software-independent format so it stays readable even after upgrades.

  • Technical reasons: better and more stable response times, more efficient database administration, shorter downtimes for upgrades, recoveries and offline backups.
  • Legal and business requirements: data privacy and protection, access for auditors, potential reuse of data.
  • Retention periods for documents, master data and transaction figures differ by country; the FI application manager agrees them with the department, auditors and other bodies, and they drive the archiving procedures, timing and retention settings.

Roles

The system administrator executes archiving. The FI application manager does not run it but coordinates with the FI department, ensures compliance with regulations, performs the FI-specific Customizing, works closely with Basis and monitors the procedure as the department's representative. Long-term goals: keep database volume roughly constant and archive proactively, not reactively.

The archiving process

  1. Write — the write program reads the data to be archived from the database and creates one or more archive files.
  2. Store — the archive files are placed in an external document storage system and administered so they can be accessed later.
  3. Delete — the deletion program reads the archive file and only then deletes the corresponding records from the database.

Writing and deleting alone is not enough; the files must be stored and managed so they remain accessible.

Key takeaways
  • Archiving removes inactive data from the database without losing read access.
  • Drivers: performance, administration effort, downtime, plus legal retention and data protection.
  • Three steps: write archive files, store them externally, delete from the database (reading the archive first).
  • Basis executes; the FI application manager coordinates, customizes and monitors.

Lesson 3: Executing Data Archiving for Specific FI Objects

Follow the FI archiving sequence — documents, transaction figures, compression run, master data — including the checks, archiving objects, programs, the special behaviour of ACDOCA in S/4HANA, and how archived data is accessed.

Process flow and dependencies

Because the universal journal (ACDOCA) is the single line item table for all of accounting, archiving objects depend on one another: an object may be archived only after its predecessors. Example: archiving ACDOCA entries without archiving Asset Accounting would leave statistical asset line items with the asset master data and break asset reporting. The course concentrates on FI-GL: FI documents → FI transaction figures → compression run → master data. Normally documents go first; transaction figures and master data follow once the document-based checks no longer block them, although a company may choose to archive only documents long-term. SAP Note 2659820 removes Material Ledger as a prerequisite in the sequence.

Running an archiving procedure (SARA → Write)

Archiving runs as a background job: it selects data objects, checks each one's archivability and writes qualifying objects to the archive file; if configured, deletion starts automatically when a file is closed, otherwise the deletion job is scheduled manually by selecting the archive files to process.

  1. Archiving variant — defines the data selection and whether it is a test or production run; variants can usually be reused only after the related jobs are deleted.
  2. Execution user — needs authorisation object S_ARCHIVE (restrictable per archiving object), authorisations for the underlying transactions, and background authorisation S_BTCH_JOB.
  3. Start time — as for standard background jobs.
  4. Spool parameters — logs can be long, so avoid immediate output to a device.

Monitoring: job logs and spool lists, CCMS monitor sets, the Fiori app Monitor Archiving Jobs, SM37 (reachable via Job Overview in SARA) and the short log under Administration in SARA. The default log records archived object count, affected tables, processed entries and file sizes; application-specific logs can go down to document level. Because spool clean-up deletes logs, decide where to keep them (e.g. external storage).

Compression run

Document archiving only flags ACDOCA rows, and transaction-figure archiving even adds rows. The compression run (FINS_ARC, monitor FINS_ARC_MONITOR) is the first step that actually shrinks ACDOCA, without changing the end-user business view. Qualifying entries (e.g. documents already archived, see SAP Note 2346296) are replaced by an aggregate whose granularity follows the balance carryforward settings (Financial Accounting → General Ledger Accounting → Periodic Processing → Carry Forward → Enter Detail Specifications for Balance Sheet Accounts / for P&L Accounts) combined with the posting period. With SAP Note 2518769 the run writes its identification into ACDOCA-SGTXT. If the aggregate nets to zero (documents and transaction figures both archived), the old entries are deleted and no aggregate is created. After document archiving the effect is memory reduction; after transaction-figure archiving it also fulfils data-protection deletion requirements. The SAP Universal Journal Detailed Analysis Service and DVM Cloud Solutions help analyse ACDOCA content and plan the steps.

Archiving FI master data

G/L accounts, customers, vendors and bank data can be archived once the business agrees and legal requirements are met. Typical path: set a posting block when the record is no longer needed, then manually set the deletion flag; the flag is one of the conditions the archiving program checks (so only responsible staff should be authorised to set it). Other checks for G/L accounts include whether documents and transaction figures still exist. There is no default minimum runtime of 999 days, and master data runs can be restricted to subsets. In S/4HANA customer/vendor data lives in the business partner, so customer/vendor master data must be archived before the business partner object CA_BUPA.

  • FI_DOCUMNT checks runtimes, minimum duration, 455 days for withholding tax, no open items; excludes recurring, parked, sample documents.
  • Deleting documents leaves ACDOCA rows with BSTAT = C and writes KNCX_DIF/LFCX_DIF totals; only the compression run (FINS_ARC) physically reduces ACDOCA.
  • Transaction figures: FI_TF_CRE / FI_TF_DEB / FI_TF_GLF, at least two years, closed periods, for data protection.
  • Master data needs a deletion flag; customer/vendor before CA_BUPA.
