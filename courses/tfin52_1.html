Unit 1: Organizational Structures

Unit objectives: Understand the organizational building blocks that classic Asset Accounting (FI-AA) in SAP ERP ECC relies on – client, company code, business area, chart of accounts and chart of depreciation – and learn how depreciation areas, CO objects and asset classes connect the asset subledger to the general ledger and to Management Accounting.

Lesson 1: Assigning a Chart of Accounts and a Chart of Depreciation to a Company Code

Learn what the FI-AA organizational units are, what a chart of depreciation is, and how a company code becomes usable for Asset Accounting by receiving a chart of depreciation.

Organizational units relevant to FI-AA

UnitMeaning for FI-AA
ClientTop of the system hierarchy; settings here apply to every company code. Charts of depreciation and asset classes exist at this level.
Company codeSelf-contained accounting unit that produces the legally required balance sheet and P&L. Each company code uses exactly one chart of accounts and one chart of depreciation.
Business areaInternal reporting unit for which an internal balance sheet and P&L can be produced. Must be defined first under Enterprise Structure → Definition → Financial Accounting → Define Business Area.

The course notes that with new General Ledger Accounting active, document splitting is what makes complete balances possible for business areas (profit center and segment are the standard characteristics for which this works out of the box). Document splitting is deliberately not activated for the training company codes (AA##), because it is irrelevant for understanding how FI-AA works; the activation status is visible with FAGL_ACTIVATION. New G/L does, however, offer an alternative way of handling parallel accounting (ledger approach), which forces a different FI-AA configuration – this is covered in Unit 4 and in course AC210.

Chart of accounts versus chart of depreciation

The chart of accounts holds all G/L accounts; FI-AA simply reuses the operative chart of accounts assigned to the company code in FI. It can be shaped for global, industry or country needs.

The chart of depreciation is the FI-AA-specific structure. It bundles the valuation rules a country requires for depreciating and valuing assets, and because these rules are country-specific, SAP ships sample charts of depreciation per country (0DE for Germany, 0US for the USA, 0GB for the UK, and so on). You never use a sample directly; you copy it into your own chart of depreciation and adapt it. Several company codes may share one chart of depreciation and one chart of accounts, but every company code has exactly one of each.

Within a chart of depreciation, each depreciation area (two-digit numeric key) represents one valuation view. Area 01 is the leading depreciation area and normally mirrors local accounting principles; it has a special role throughout the course. Further areas can represent:

  • tax balance sheet valuation
  • cost-accounting (costing-based) valuation
  • valuation in another currency or according to another approach, e.g. group valuation / IFRS / US GAAP for consolidated statements
  • capital tax valuation
  • derived differences between book and tax depreciation

The point of having several areas is that the same asset portfolio must be valued differently for different audiences: local book depreciation, tax authorities (where a different valuation is allowed), internal cost accounting, and parallel accounting for a group balance sheet.

Training-system specifics: in Germany the exercises copy chart of depreciation 1AC (a copy of 0DE plus a few extra values) because the 0DE in the training system differs from the standard; US classes may use 1ACU (near copy of 0US).

Making a company code an FI-AA company code

  1. Create and configure the company code in FI (global parameters, fiscal year variant, etc.).
  2. Assign a chart of depreciation to it – ideally as a separate project step.
  3. Enrich the company code with the remaining FI-AA Customizing (number ranges, account assignment, screen layouts, …).

Only then can assets be created and posted for that company code. A company code without a chart of depreciation cannot use FI-AA at all – which may be intentional, e.g. a small company code that manages its fixed assets in an external system.

Key takeaways
  • Client → company code → business area; the company code is the legal reporting unit and the anchor for FI-AA.
  • Chart of accounts = G/L accounts (shared with FI); chart of depreciation = country-specific valuation rules, made of depreciation areas.
  • Charts of depreciation live at client level; copy a country sample (0XY), never use it directly; many company codes can share one.
  • Area 01 is the leading area and posts to the G/L in real time.
  • Without an assigned chart of depreciation a company code cannot run FI-AA.

Lesson 2: Assigning Management Accounting Objects to Fixed Assets

See which Controlling objects can be stored in an asset master record and which of them can receive the depreciation that FI-AA posts.

CO objects in the asset master

FI-AA is integrated with Management Accounting through account assignment objects in the asset master record. The CO objects belong to the controlling area, so they may be valid for one or several company codes. The "genuine" CO objects you can enter are:

ObjectNature of the assignment
Cost centerReal account assignment; the usual receiver of depreciation.
Internal orderReal or statistical.
Activity typePurely statistical information.

Objects from other applications that carry controlling functionality can be assigned as well: WBS elements, real estate objects, maintenance orders (statistical only) and Public Sector Management objects.

Where depreciation can be posted

Depreciation from any depreciation area can flow to: a cost center; a real order; cost center plus statistical order; a WBS element; cost center plus statistical WBS element; a real estate object; or PSM objects. A single asset can never be split between two cost centers. If an asset's cost must be shared, assign it to a real internal order and settle that order periodically to the receiving cost centers.

Key takeaways
  • Cost center, internal order and activity type are the original CO objects in the asset master; cost elements are not assigned there.
  • Activity type and maintenance order are statistical only.
  • One asset → at most one cost center; use an order with settlement to distribute depreciation.

Lesson 3: Defining How Depreciation Areas Post to the General Ledger

Decide, per depreciation area, which values (APC, depreciation, none) are transferred to FI and how, and link each area to a financial statement version for asset reporting.

Why not every area posts

FI-AA is a subledger: each asset carries values in several depreciation areas, but financial statements are only needed for some of them. Customizing therefore defines for each area whether and how values reach the G/L (classic "accounts approach", where each parallel valuation has its own set of G/L accounts).

IndicatorPosting behaviour
0Area does not post to FI (reporting only).
1Posts asset values (APC) in real time; depreciation periodically. Mandatory for area 01.
2Posts asset values and depreciation periodically.
3Posts depreciation only, periodically.
4Posts asset values directly (immediately) and depreciation periodically.

Area 01 posts APC online. Traditionally area 01 represents local GAAP, but increasingly companies put the international accounting principle (IFRS/US GAAP) into area 01 so that it is the view updated in real time. Areas other than 01 can push APC to FI either periodically through program RAPERB2000 (periodic APC posting) or directly (indicator 4). Depreciation is never posted in real time – it is always a periodic run with RAPOST2000.

Financial statement versions for asset reports

Values of different areas can be routed to different balance sheet and P&L accounts, and any number of financial statement versions (FSV) can be defined per chart of accounts in G/L Customizing. To let asset reports group assets by financial statement item, each depreciation area must know which FSV applies: IMG Financial Accounting (New) → Asset Accounting → Integration with General Ledger Accounting → Specify Financial Statement Version for Asset Reports. The report then shows the items when you select a sort variant containing the financial statement item field (structure ANLAV, field ERGSO).

ActivityIMG path (Financial Accounting (New) → Asset Accounting → …)
Set the posting indicator per areaIntegration with General Ledger Accounting → Define How Depreciation Areas Post to General Ledger

Course example (chart AA##): area 01 posts in real time, area 20 (cost-accounting) posts only depreciation, all other areas do not update the G/L. Areas 30/31 are the consolidated-balance-sheet areas and receive FSV CAUS; the others get INT. Areas 31 and 32, which report in group currency, are set to USD.

Key takeaways
  • Posting indicators 0–4 control what each depreciation area sends to FI; area 01 must post APC in real time.
  • Depreciation is always periodic (RAPOST2000); periodic APC of non-leading areas uses RAPERB2000.
  • Reporting-only areas (indicator 0) are perfectly valid.
  • Assign an FSV to every area so asset reports can show financial statement items via sort variant field ERGSO.
  • Depreciation areas can carry their own currency, e.g. group currency USD.

Lesson 4: Understanding the Importance of Asset Classes in Fixed Assets

Understand why the asset class is the central classification and control object for fixed assets and what it carries.

Role of the asset class

Every asset belongs to exactly one asset class (buildings, vehicles, fixtures and fittings, machines, …). Asset classes are defined at client level and are the main criterion for classifying assets: assets that appear under different balance sheet items (buildings vs. machines) must be in different classes. Special classes exist for assets under construction, low-value assets, intangibles such as patents or software, and leased assets.

Structure of an asset class

An asset class has two main sections:

  • Master data section – default values for master record fields plus control data: account determination, number range interval and screen layout rule.
  • Depreciation data section – per depreciation area, proposed depreciation terms (key, useful life, …). Individual areas can be deactivated for a class, and proposed terms can be overwritten when an asset is created.

Because one asset class can be linked to several charts of depreciation, a group can keep a globally uniform class catalog while depreciation rules differ per country.

Scope note: FI-AA covers the financial valuation of tangible/intangible assets. Technical asset management belongs to Plant Maintenance (Enterprise Asset Management); financial instruments are handled in Treasury and Risk Management (Accounting → Financial Supply Chain Management).

Key takeaways
  • Asset class = main classification criterion; one asset ↔ one class; classes exist client-wide.
  • Control data in the class: account determination, number range, screen layout; plus default depreciation terms per area.
  • A class can be assigned to many charts of depreciation, supporting a uniform global catalog.
  • Insurance type is not a control element of the class.
Practice No hands-on exercise in this lesson. Unit assessment questions check: company code as independent accounting unit; which CO objects go in the asset master (activity type, order, cost center – not cost element); no double cost-center assignment; FSV per area; asset class control data; class usable with several charts of depreciation; classes at client level.

Unit 2: Master Data

Unit objectives: Take the asset class apart – account determination, number ranges, screen layout rules for master data and depreciation areas, user fields, and the special AuC and low-value-asset classes – then use it to create, change and mass-change asset master records in classic FI-AA, including subnumbers, time-dependent data and the link to PM equipment.

Lesson 1: Explaining the Components of an Asset Class

Understand each control element inside an asset class – account determination, number range, screen layouts, user-field defaults – and the particularities of the AuC and LVA classes, so that the class can act as a reliable template for future asset master records.

Account determination

The account determination key in the class is the bridge between an asset's values and the G/L accounts that receive its APC and depreciation postings. One key can serve different strategies:

  • With a very small class catalog the key could simply equal the balance sheet account number (rare in practice).
  • Several similar classes can share one key and therefore post to one balance sheet account.
  • If company codes use different operative charts of accounts, a single key is enough: the G/L accounts are maintained per chart of accounts behind the same key.

Accounts are maintained under Financial Accounting (New) → Asset Accounting → Integration with General Ledger Accounting → Assign G/L Accounts (AO90), per chart of accounts and account determination. For every area that posts depreciation you need extra accounts: accumulated depreciation, depreciation expense and write-up revenue for ordinary and unplanned depreciation, optionally accounts for revaluation and for interest in the cost-accounting area. Course example in chart INT: key 20000 (class 2000/2100) posts acquisitions to balance sheet account 11000 and cost-accounting depreciation (area 20) to expense account 481000.

Number assignment

TypeBehaviour
InternalSystem takes the next free number of the interval assigned to the class.
ExternalUser or an external system supplies the number.

Intervals are defined per company code (Organizational Structures → Asset Classes → Define Number Range Interval, AS08; quickest setup is Utilities → Copy from an existing company code such as 1000). Alternatively a company code can borrow the intervals of another one via Specify Number Assignment Across Company Codes (field No. CoCd) – convenient but then the ranges are shared.

Screen layout rule for master data

Each class references a screen layout rule (maintained under Master Data → Screen Layout → Define Screen Layout for Asset Master Data), which works on logical field groups (General data, Posting information, Time-dependent data, Allocations, Insurance, …) and controls three things per field:

ControlMeaning
Field selectionRequired / optional / display / suppressed – trims the master record to what the class needs and forces key control data.
Maintenance levelWhere the field may be maintained: asset class, main asset number or subnumber.
ReferenceWhether the field content is copied when an asset is created with reference to another asset.

Course findings: class 2100 uses rule 2000; under rule 3000 "Description 1" is not copied from the reference, whereas Insurance Type is.

Additional account assignment objects (cost center, order, WBS element, real estate object, PSM objects, and from EHP5 profit center and segment) only appear in the master record after being activated under Integration with General Ledger Accounting → Additional Account Assignment Objects → Activate Account Assignment Objects; the screen layout then decides whether they are mandatory. Besides cost-accounting depreciation, APC can also be posted statistically to investment orders or WBS elements – needed for the direct capitalization scenario of Investment Management and for budget monitoring of asset purchases.

The tab layout (number and names of tab pages and which logical field groups sit on each) can also be defined per asset class.

Screen layout for depreciation areas

In the depreciation data section (OAYZ, Valuation → Determine Depreciation Areas in the Asset Class) each area of the class gets its own screen layout rule – SAP delivers 1000 and 2000 – plus proposed depreciation key and useful life, and areas can be deactivated. The rule's maintenance level fixes how uniformly valuation is controlled:

Maintenance levelEffect
Asset classTerms fixed in the class; identical for all assets of the class.
Main asset numberClass values are proposed and may be changed in the master record; subnumbers inherit from the main number.
Asset subnumberEach subnumber may have its own depreciation terms.

Course findings: class 2100 proposes key LINK in all areas except area 20 (LINA); class 3000 uses rule 1000 in all areas; in rule 1000 the depreciation key is a required field. The course works at class level (OAYZ) and asset level (AS02).

Further class functions

  • Copy class from reference (OAOA, Edit → Copy As): copies master data section and depreciation section; a class created without reference has only the master data section and the depreciation section must be configured afterwards.
  • User fields with definable allowed values, usable as report selection criteria: evaluation groups 1–5 (1–4 are four characters, 5 is eight), environmental protection indicator, reason for investment, asset super number (groups several assets, e.g. a production line).
  • Default values in the class: user fields, insurance, net worth tax, leasing, depreciation key / useful life / index series per area.

Special classes: AuC and LVA

Assets under construction need their own class and G/L account because they are shown separately in the balance sheet. Depreciation key 0000 prevents depreciation in the balance sheet areas; special tax depreciation and investment support remain possible; down payments can be posted through AP; and credit memos after full capitalization work only if negative APC is allowed in the area detail screen. The class setting "Status of AuC" decides between summary settlement and line-item settlement – class 4000 in the training system settles by line item. Larger investments are handled with investment measures (orders/projects) in Investment Management.

Low-value assets are checked against a country-specific maximum amount. Individual management and collective management (several items in one record, requiring a base unit of measure in OAOA) need separate classes. Maximum amounts per company code/area and the check type per class are set under Valuation → Amount Specifications (Company Code/Depreciation Area) → Specify Max. Amount for Low-Value Assets + Asset Classes; separate limits exist for normal postings and purchase-order acquisitions. In class, area 15 of AA## gets EUR 1,000 and class 3005 checks against the value-based maximum.

Key takeaways
  • Account determination (per chart of accounts) links class → G/L accounts for APC, depreciation, write-ups, revaluation, interest.
  • Number range intervals are company-code specific, internal or external, or shared across company codes.
  • Screen layout rules govern field status, maintenance level and reference copying; depreciation areas have their own rules (1000/2000) whose maintenance level sets how far terms may deviate from the class.
  • Copying a class copies both sections; class 4000 (AuC) uses key 0000 and line-item settlement; LVA classes check a maximum amount and may be managed individually or collectively.
  • Account assignment objects must be activated before they appear in the master record.

Lesson 2: Creating and Changing Master Data in Asset Accounting

Create asset master records (from a class, from a reference, in bulk, as subnumbers), maintain time-dependent data, trace changes, and understand the FI-AA ↔ PM equipment link.

Ways to create an asset

  1. From an asset class (AS01): enter company code and class; the class supplies the defaults; fill required fields; on save the system assigns the number.
  2. From a reference asset: often gives better defaults than the class alone, but beware of copying unwanted data such as the capitalization date (the screen layout's reference flags decide what is copied).
  3. Multiple similar assets: enter "Number of similar records" on the initial screen; after saving, the Maintain pushbutton lets you vary description, inventory number, business area, cost center and evaluation groups 1–5 per asset before creating them.

Subnumbers

An asset consisting of components can be split into subnumbers (AS11, also with "number of similar subnumbers"). Typical reasons: separately tracking subsequent acquisitions in later years (e.g. buildings), managing component values separately, or technical splits. The class decides whether subnumbers are numbered internally or externally (external is handy during legacy transfer, switch to internal afterwards), and the screen layout's maintenance level decides whether subnumbers may carry their own depreciation terms.

Personal value list

Since release 4.6 frequently used assets appear in a personal value list on F4. It can be disabled via AS02 → Settings → Interface; existing entries must be deleted before the general search help shows again.

Time-dependent data

Assignments to CO objects (cost center, project), shift factor and shutdown indicator are stored as time intervals because they influence cost allocation and depreciation. On the Time-dependent tab, "More Intervals" → "Add interval" creates a new validity period from a given date; the course moves a photocopier from cost center T-F05A## to T-F05B## for 1 June – 31 October and back on 1 November.

Change documents

Every master record change produces a change document (changed fields, number of changes per field, user, old/new values), viewable from AS02/AS03 via Environment → Change documents → On asset, or with report RAAEND01 (Information System → Preparations for closing → International → Changes to Asset Master Records). Many assets at once → mass change (Lesson 3).

Depreciation areas in the master record

The asset's depreciation section is filled from the class and can be adjusted per area (key, useful life, depreciation start). Some values come from the acquisition posting; extra parameters such as index series, variable depreciation portion and scrap value are mainly relevant for the cost-accounting area. SAP ERP 6.0 allows selected depreciation terms to be time-dependent.

Asset and equipment (PM)

Several PM equipment records may point to one asset, but each equipment belongs to one asset only. Since 4.6C the two can be kept synchronous: an equipment can be created automatically with the asset (copying e.g. company code and inventory number), and changes on either side update the other; a workflow can notify the PM manager. Configuration: Master Data → Automatic Creation of Equipment Master Records (SAP Notes 370884, 549929).

Key takeaways
  • AS01 create, AS02 change, AS03 display, AS11 subnumber; mass creation via "number of similar records".
  • Reference creation copies only fields flagged as reference fields – check the capitalization date.
  • Cost center, shift and shutdown are time-dependent intervals; every change is logged in a change document (RAAEND01).
  • Equipment ↔ asset synchronization integrates FI-AA with Plant Maintenance.
  • Report S_ALR_87012056 / RAANLA_ALV01 lists unposted assets.

Lesson 3: Carrying Out Mass Changes to Asset Master Data Using a Worklist

Change many asset master records at once by combining a substitution rule with a worklist generated from an asset report.

Concept

Mass changes run from the FI-AA application menu (Environment → Worklist) and only need the application authorization – no workflow task assignment in Customizing is required. The change logic is a substitution rule with two parts: prerequisites (conditions selecting the records, built in the form editor or in expert mode using table/field names) and substitutions (constant values, field-to-field assignments or user exits). SAP Note 210897 covers substitutions for time-dependent data.

Procedure

  1. Define the substitution (OA02 → Substitution → Create), insert a step, pick the field (e.g. Evaluation group 3 = ANLA-ORD43), set the prerequisite (e.g. asset class = MA##) and the constant value.
  2. Assign the substitution to the company code (same transaction, New Entries: company code, sequence number, substitution).
  3. Run any asset report (e.g. Directory of Unposted Assets) or Environment → Worklist → Generate, select the assets, choose Create Worklist.
  4. Give the worklist a name and a purpose (standard task such as "Change asset w/o dialog (bulk change)"), choose the substitution; the system returns a work queue number.
  5. Edit and release the worklist with AR31 (Environment → Worklist → Edit), refresh until status Completed.
  6. Verify with AS03 or a report.
Key takeaways
  • Mass change = substitution rule (prerequisite + substitution) applied to a worklist of assets.
  • OA02 maintains rules and their company-code assignment; AR31 processes worklists.
  • Worklists are generated from any asset report's result list.
  • Changing a class default does not touch existing assets – that is what the mass change is for.
Practice Exercise 12: define evaluation group 3 value 99## (Master Data → User Fields → Define 4-Character Evaluation Groups), store it as default in class MA## (Enter Your User Fields in Asset Class) and confirm the proposal on the Allocations tab in AS01; then create substitution Z## in OA02 (prerequisite class = MA##, constant 99## for ORD43), assign it to AA##, build a worklist from the Directory of Unposted Assets (sort variant 0007) with task "Change asset w/o dialog", release it in AR31, and check in AS03 that only MA## assets changed.

Unit 3: Asset Transactions

Unit objectives: Master the day-to-day business transactions of classic FI-AA in ECC – integrated and non-integrated acquisitions (including MM and the profit center/segment characteristics), retirements, intracompany and intercompany transfers, settlement of assets under construction and unplanned depreciation – and learn to read the results in the Asset Explorer and the asset history sheet.

Lesson 1: Creating Acquisitions

Post an external asset acquisition integrated with Accounts Payable, understand what the posting does to the asset master and the FI document, and analyze the result in the Asset Explorer.

FI-AA as a subledger

Like AP and AR, Asset Accounting is a subledger: every posting to an asset simultaneously updates the G/L reconciliation (balance sheet) account determined through account determination, so subledger and G/L are always reconciled. The reconciliation accounts must be set up jointly with the asset department before go-live.

Ways to post an external acquisition

In-house production is capitalized via an Investment Management measure (order/project) settled to an AuC and then to the final asset. Less common: a CO order already charged with the invoice is settled to the asset.

Transaction types

Every asset posting carries a transaction type (e.g. 100 external acquisition, 030 acquisition in group area). It tells the asset history sheet and other reports what kind of movement it is and whether it concerns current-year or prior-year values, and can be restricted to specific depreciation areas. Transaction types are grouped into fixed transaction type groups; SAP delivers all needed types, customers may copy them in Customizing under Asset Accounting → Transactions.

Document splitting remark

With new G/L, document splitting can build complete balance sheets per profit center, business area or segment (the standard splitting characteristics). It is not part of FI-AA logic and is not active in company code AA## (see AC210).

Asset Explorer (AW01N)

Central analysis screen: a tree of depreciation areas (icons distinguish real and derived areas), header with company code/main number/subnumber, a jump to master data, tabs for planned values, posted values, comparison across years and areas, and parameters; the "objects related to the asset" area links to cost center, G/L account, vendor, purchase order, equipment. Functions: transparent depreciation calculation with key details, jump to FI document, multi-year/multi-area value development, currency translation, call-up of asset reports, print/export, and simulation of transactions and changed depreciation terms.

Effect of the first acquisition on the master record

  • Capitalization date and date of initial acquisition are set from the asset value date.
  • Acquisition year and period come from the posting date.
  • The ordinary depreciation start date per area is derived from the asset value date through the period control of the depreciation key and stored in the master record.
  • With AP integration the vendor is written to the Origin tab.
  • Default asset value dates per transaction can be set in Customizing. Posting date and asset value date must lie in the same fiscal year.

Planned annual depreciation and interest are computed from the depreciation start date and the terms and updated with each further posting.

FI document basics

The document type (two characters, client-wide; defaults via OBU1) carries the number range key, the allowed account types and the gross/net indicator. Number range intervals themselves are company-code specific (FBN1; use year 9999 if not year-dependent). "Net" document types (AN, KN) deduct the expected cash discount from the capitalized amount immediately via a cash discount clearing account; "gross" types (AA, KR) capitalize the full amount. Without document splitting, program SAPF181 (P&L adjustment) later corrects the APC for discount actually taken or not taken; with active document splitting the correction is immediate and SAPF181 is unnecessary.

Key takeaways

Lesson 2: Posting Asset Acquisitions with the Profit Center and Segment Characteristics

Understand how profit center and segment get into asset postings, what EHP5 "Segment Reporting" for FI-AA changes, how to configure it, and how to fill the new fields for assets that already exist.

Background

With new G/L, profit center, segment and business area are stored in the G/L totals and balance sheets can be produced for them (with document splitting). For asset postings these characteristics must therefore be carried in the FI document.

Before EHP5: derivation from the CO object

Up to SAP ERP 6.0 EHP4 the asset master had no profit center/segment fields; the FI document derived them from the asset's CO object (cost center, order). This required the account assignment type "APC Values Posting" for the CO objects in the APC-posting area (01) under Integration with General Ledger Accounting → Additional Account Assignment Objects → Specify Account Assignment Types for Account Assignment Objects (ACSET). Weaknesses: no uniqueness check in the master data, G/L–subledger reconciliation by profit center not guaranteed (substitutions could change the profit center in the document; the CO object's profit center could be changed without any FI correction), and internal logic decided when several CO objects with different profit centers were assigned.

EHP5: Segment Reporting for fixed assets

  1. Activate business function FIN_GL_REORG_1 in SFW5 (requires new G/L; irreversible; also enables profit center reorganization, which needs an SAP LT license only if used – SAP Note 1534197).
  2. Activate Integration with General Ledger Accounting → Segment Reporting → Activate Segment Reporting. Profit center and segment then appear in the table of additional account assignment objects (XACTIVE) with the Agreement indicator (XIDENT) set, meaning the value used in the posting must equal the master record value and cannot be changed during posting.
  3. The fields become available in the screen layout (logical field group Time-dependent data) and can be suppressed if not required (e.g. segment).
  4. Maintain account assignment type "APC Values Posting" for profit center and segment in every area that posts APC (ACSET); otherwise derivation fails and even the earlier settings for cost center/order stop working.
  5. Ensure the field status of the asset G/L accounts and of the posting keys has profit center and segment as optional (IMG nodes under Change the Field Status Variant of the Asset G/L Accounts).

Technically EHP4 already added the fields to table ANLZ; EHP5 plus business function plus activation are needed to see them. In the master record the profit center is derived from the cost center and the segment from the profit center (check with KS03/KE53); entering a second CO object with a different profit center raises error AIST009. New G/L scenario FIN_AASEG is only needed if FIN_CONS is not assigned – it stores the consolidation transaction type (RMVCT) in FAGLFLEXT, enabling an asset-history-sheet-like Power List in G/L. Segment cannot be shown in the Asset Explorer (not a BOR object), but both fields are available for FI-AA validations/substitutions and BW.

Existing assets: FAGL_ASSET_MASTERDATA_UPD

After activation all existing assets have empty profit center/segment and, if APC account assignment types are maintained, cannot be posted to. Either maintain time-dependent data manually (AS02, e.g. re-enter the business area to trigger derivation) or run program FAGL_ASSET_MASTERDATA_UPD (Segment Reporting → Fill Master Data for Segment Reporting): test mode online, productive run in background only; two spool lists show from which CO object the values were derived and which assets were updated (green) or not (red). Prerequisites: "Reorganization for Fixed Assets" inactive and the Balance sheet flag (XBILA) for profit center/segment inactive. Old FI documents are not corrected by this program.

Key takeaways
  • Pre-EHP5 derivation from CO objects is error-prone; EHP5 stores profit center and segment in the asset master (ANLZ) with the Agreement indicator enforcing consistency.
  • Activation chain: FIN_GL_REORG_1 → Activate Segment Reporting → screen layout → ACSET APC Values Posting for PC/segment → field status.
  • Error AIST009 flags non-unique profit centers across CO objects in one asset.
  • FAGL_ASSET_MASTERDATA_UPD fills the new fields for legacy assets (background productive run).

Lesson 3: Posting Non-Integrated Asset Acquisitions and Understanding the Integration with Materials Management (MM)

Post acquisitions through a clearing account when invoice and asset arrive at different times, and follow the purchase order – goods receipt – invoice receipt chain that capitalizes an asset from MM.

Acquisition with automatic offsetting entry

When the invoice arrives before the asset, or the asset is in use before the invoice exists, the two halves are posted separately through a G/L clearing account with open item management: AP posts debit clearing / debit tax / credit vendor; FI-AA posts debit asset / credit clearing (ABZON). The clearing account is cleared afterwards manually or with program SAPF124, or immediately with F-91 (Clearing Offsetting Entry) when the second half is entered. Since release 4.6 ABZON can also create the asset master during posting (New Asset radio button) and post to several assets or to a main number with all its subnumbers in one step (Multiple assets / Subnumbers pushbuttons). Asset documents are reversed with AB08; LVA postings above the configured maximum are rejected, and a subsequent acquisition on an LVA fails with an explanatory error.

MM integration

  1. Purchase requisition, and asset master if needed (AS01).
  2. Purchase order (ME21N) with account assignment category A; the asset number is entered on the item's Account Assignment tab (the asset could even be created from the PO, rarely done in practice). On the Delivery tab the GR is flagged valuated or non-valuated.
  3. Goods receipt (MIGO): with a valuated GR the asset is capitalized at GR with the PO value; later invoice differences generate adjustment postings on the asset. With a non-valuated GR nothing is posted yet, but the GR date becomes the capitalization date.
  4. Invoice receipt (MIRO): for a non-valuated GR this capitalizes the asset, creates line items and updates values; for a valuated GR it posts the difference (course example: PO 2,000, invoice 1,995 → a –5 adjustment on the asset).

In the Asset Explorer a PO is visible under "objects related to the asset" before any value is posted; no capitalization date exists until a value posting occurs.

Key takeaways
  • ABZON = acquisition with automatic offsetting entry to a clearing account; F-91 clears the clearing account in the same step; SAPF124 clears automatically.
  • ABZON can create the asset on the fly and post to multiple assets/subnumbers at once.
  • MM chain: PO with account assignment A → GR → IR; valuated GR capitalizes at GR, non-valuated GR capitalizes at invoice but keeps the GR date as capitalization date.
  • Invoice/PO differences produce adjustment postings on a valuated asset.

Intracompany transfer (ABUMN)

Reasons: an asset was created in the wrong class, part of an asset must be split off, or an AuC is to be moved to a finished asset (for which the dedicated settlement function is more convenient). The system uses transfer variant 4; the variant determines the transaction types recorded on sending and receiving asset. Field transfer rules copy data from the old to the new master record, and the acquisition transaction type controls how the depreciation start date is carried over. Setting the asset value date to 1 January of the current year makes the receiving asset take over the full-year depreciation; the Origin tab of the new asset shows the original asset, and expired/remaining useful life are carried (course: one year expired, four remaining; value adjustment of EUR 20,000 transferred).

Lesson 6: Representing, Distributing, and Settling Assets Under Construction

Manage the construction phase of an asset in a separate AuC master record and capitalize it to one or more finished assets by line-item settlement.

Two phases, two master records

An asset is shown under different balance sheet items while under construction and during its useful life, so FI-AA uses an AuC master record (class 4000) for the first phase and completed assets for the second. Moving values across is the capitalization of the AuC. An AuC can be managed as a normal asset for summary settlement or with line item management for line-item settlement; large investment measures with internal activities are better handled in Investment Management.

Line-item settlement procedure

  1. Assign a settlement profile (standard AI) to the company code: Transactions → Capitalization of Assets under Construction → Define/Assign Settlement Profiles.
  2. Post acquisitions to the AuC (e.g. ABZON, with posting texts).
  3. AIAB (Capitalize Asset u. Construction → Distribute): list the line items (layout 3SAP shows texts), select items with the same distribution and enter distribution rules (receiver asset, percentage or amount).
  4. Execute settlement (AIBU, via Environment → Execute Settlement) in test run, then update run.

Only items with a rule are settled; you need not settle everything at once nor 100% of each item. The system credits the AuC with different transaction types for current-year and prior-year acquisitions, and the receiving assets show the capitalized values in the asset history sheet (S_ALR_87011990, sort variant 0013).

Key takeaways
  • AuC = separate balance sheet item and separate master record; capitalization transfers it to completed assets.
  • Settlement profile AI must be assigned to the company code before AIAB/AIBU can be used.
  • Distribution rules per line item allow partial and multi-receiver settlement; always test run first.
  • Different credit transaction types reflect prior-year vs. current-year acquisitions on the AuC.

Unit 4: Periodic Processing and Valuation

Lesson 1: Defining Depreciation Areas, Keys, Calculation, and Posting

Configure depreciation areas and depreciation keys, understand how the new depreciation calculation derives planned amounts (including time-dependent terms, interest and replacement values), and execute and analyze the depreciation posting run.

Periodic processing in FI-AA

Periodic tasks include: passing planned depreciation and interest to CO primary cost planning via a report; investment support (subsidies for assets flagged with an investment support key, posted manually per asset); inflation management for high-inflation countries; and the Schedule Manager for defining, scheduling and monitoring recurring jobs.

Configuring depreciation areas

Each area (two-digit numeric key) is defined under Valuation → Depreciation Areas → Define Depreciation Areas (OADB). The definition decides whether and how APC, proportional value adjustments and depreciation reach the G/L (posting indicator), whether the area is reporting-only, what purpose it serves (balance sheet, cost accounting, tax), how values may develop (APC only, positive and/or negative net book value), and how posting values and depreciation terms are inherited from other areas (Specify Transfer of APC Values / Specify Transfer of Depreciation Terms). Per area you also decide which depreciation types are allowed (Depreciation → Ordinary Depreciation → Determine Depreciation Areas, likewise for special and unplanned depreciation). Changing a term in one area may be forced one-to-one into others if a transfer rule is set – the system warns on save.

Depreciation types

TypeMeaning
OrdinaryPlanned wear-and-tear reduction of value.

Depreciation key and calculation methods

Since release 4.6 a depreciation key (AFAMA, Depreciation → Valuation Methods → Depreciation Key → Maintain Depreciation Key) is a chart-of-depreciation-specific bundle of calculation methods, replacing the old internal calculation keys:

New depreciation calculation (Depreciation Engine)

With SAP ERP 6.0 and Enterprise Extension EA-FIN active, depreciation is calculated per period interval instead of per transaction. The engine looks at how long one reference value (APC or net book value) is valid in the year: no transactions → one interval (periods 1–12); each transaction or term change opens a new interval according to the period control. Results often equal the old logic (especially in Germany, where subsequent acquisitions are treated as of the start of the year), but can differ where pro-rata treatment is used (e.g. Japan) – see SAP Notes 965032 and 1121965. Transaction AW01_AFAR shows what the old logic would compute. An automatic changeover method is not standard but possible via BAdI FAA_DC_CUSTOMER.

Time-dependent depreciation terms: depreciation key, useful life (years/periods), variable depreciation portion, absolute and percentage scrap value can be maintained in intervals (detail screen of the area in AS02 → More Intervals / Add Interval), analogous to time-dependent master data; the intervals are visible in the Asset Explorer (SAP Notes 1398629, 981222). Without time-dependent terms a change affects all open and future years; with them only future periods from the interval start. Changes that violate area rules are silently cut back ("reduction of calculated depreciation") – e.g. shortening the useful life only in area 01 would make the derived area 03 (= 02 − 01) positive, which its definition forbids, so the change has no effect.

Cost-accounting depreciation area

Area 20 may calculate imputed interest and may depreciate below zero / after the planned useful life (settings in the area definition). Typical terms: key LINA (straight-line from replacement value, pro rata, with curb and interest), index series indexing APC (e.g. 103%, effective from the second year), interest 10% on half the APC, depreciation after planned life end, depreciation below NBV zero (area must allow negative NBV, possibly via a changeover key), and the curb – after the planned life the rate drops from 1/10 to 1/11, 1/12 … so depreciation slowly decreases. Imputed interest requires: area allows interest, company code/area set to post interest (posting rules), a key with interest calculation methods; interest is posted with the depreciation run to the accounts of the area's account determination and additionally to the asset's cost center or order. Index series (Special Valuation → Revaluation of Fixed Assets → Indexed Replacement Values → Define Index Series, index class) hold index figures per fiscal year; if missing the system uses a simulated annual rate. The series is defaulted from the class or set in the asset's Valuation tab; revaluation can also apply to accumulated depreciation and interest, and you choose whether to post APC revaluation only or also depreciation/interest to the G/L.

Depreciation run (RAPOST2000)

Periodic Processing → Depreciation Run → Execute (AFAB) posts ordinary, special (incl. reserves), unplanned and manual depreciation, imputed interest and revaluation, writing FI documents directly to the G/L plus CO documents for additional account assignments. Run types: planned posting run, repeat run, restart, unplanned posting run (e.g. several periods at once). The test run performs all checks (unlike old RABUCH00) and lists errors with red lights: locked cost centers, missing account assignment types ("account requires assignment to a CO object"), missing accounts, wrong period relative to the posting cycle, missing posting cycle settings; simulated FI documents can be opened from the log. The productive log is RAPOST2001 (Display Log), with a jump to the Schedule Manager monitor; the Asset Explorer's Posted Values tab shows posted periods and links to the depreciation document.

Prerequisite settings:

  1. Area definition allows posting of depreciation (Define Depreciation Areas).
  2. Depreciation accounts in account determination (Assign G/L Accounts).
  3. Document type for depreciation (standard AF) per company code (Post Depreciation to General Ledger Accounting → Specify Document Type for Posting of Depreciation; external number ranges: SAP Note 890976).
  4. Intervals and posting rules per area (OAYR): monthly/quarterly/semi-annual/annual, plus flags such as post interest, post revaluation.
  5. Activate account assignment objects (cost center KOSTL, order CAUFN, WBS element PS_PSP_PNR2, profit center PRCTR, segment …).
  6. Account assignment type Depreciation Run per company code/area/object (ACSET); with segment reporting active, PRCTR and SEGMENT need it explicitly in every depreciating area. AACCOBJ / RAACCOBJ01 lists active objects.
Key takeaways
  • Depreciation key = base + declining-balance + maximum amount + multilevel + period control methods (AFAMA); key changes need AFAR/AS02 recalculation.
  • Area definition governs posting indicator, allowed depreciation types, NBV rules and transfer of terms/values between areas.
  • EA-FIN Depreciation Engine calculates by period intervals and enables time-dependent key, useful life, variable portion and scrap value.
  • Area 20: interest, index series/replacement value, curb and depreciation below zero.
  • RAPOST2000 (AFAB) needs document type AF, posting rules (OAYR), accounts and account assignment types (ACSET); test run catches errors; log via RAPOST2001.

Lesson 2: Executing Programs for Fiscal Year Change and Year-End Closing in Asset Accounting

Distinguish the technical fiscal year change from the accounting year-end close in FI-AA and run both, including the periodic APC posting that must precede the close.

Fiscal year change (RAJAWE00, AJRW)

Opens new annual value fields for every asset of a company code so that postings in the new year are possible. It may be started in the last period of the current year, must be run for the whole company code, and can only move to year N+1 if year N−1 is already closed. It has nothing to do with closing the books.

Year-end closing (RAJABS00, AJAB)

Preparation: check depreciation lists and the asset history sheet, post depreciation completely, run RAPERB2000 in update mode at least once for areas that post APC periodically, correct via simulation, mass changes or adjustment postings, and re-run depreciation if values changed. AJAB then checks that depreciation and APC are fully posted and that no asset is incomplete or erroneous; if clean, it records the last closed fiscal year per depreciation area and blocks asset postings to closed years. Re-opening a year (Undo → Entire Company Code, OAAQ; per area OAAR) requires running AJAB again to close it. Path: Periodic Processing → Year-End Closing → Execute.

Periodic APC posting (RAPERB2000, ASKB)

Since release 4.7 EE 1.10 / ECC 5.0 the program posts APC of periodically posting areas straight to the (group) balance sheet accounts without a batch-input session. It needs a dedicated document type with internal numbering (SAP delivers none; the course uses "AP"): define it under Post Depreciation to General Ledger Accounting → Specify Document Type for Posting of Depreciation → Define Document Types, create the number range interval via Number Range Information (SAP Note 890976), and assign it under Post APC Values Periodically to General Ledger Accounting → Specify Document Type for Periodic Posting of Asset Values. The test run yields an ALV log with document simulation. Without the document type the program refuses to start, and without a completed update run the year-end close reports that periodic postings are still open.

Key takeaways
  • Fiscal year change (AJRW) is technical – it opens value fields; year-end closing (AJAB) is the accounting close.
  • AJAB only succeeds when depreciation and periodic APC (ASKB/RAPERB2000) are fully posted; it sets the last closed year and blocks postings.

Lesson 3: Using the Accounts Approach for Parallel Accounting in Asset Accounting (FI-AA)

Represent a second accounting principle (IFRS, US GAAP, tax) in FI-AA with the accounts approach: an extra depreciation area posting to a separate set of G/L accounts, either periodically or directly.

Mapping options

With new G/L you may choose the ledger approach or the accounts approach; with classic G/L only the accounts approach is available, which is why this course covers it. The accounts approach is SAP-recommended as purely account-based and supported by all applications, but it is maintenance-heavy: an additional set of group accounts (often one extra digit in the chart of accounts), two separate balance sheet/P&L structures, an additional depreciation area that posts APC periodically or directly, and the periodic program RAPERB2000.

  3. Assign the periodic APC document type (AP) to the company code.
  4. Activate area 60 in the asset classes (Determine Depreciation Areas in the Asset Class, remove Deact flag, set default useful life e.g. 5 years).

Lesson 4: Processing Impairment Postings in Subledger Assets

Record an IFRS/US GAAP impairment on the assets of a cash-generating unit using the FI-AA worklist task "Impairment Posting", implemented technically as revaluation posted by the depreciation run.

Business background

If an asset's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount, it must be impaired. The recoverable amount is determined for the single asset or, if not possible, for its cash-generating unit (CGU) – the smallest identifiable group of assets generating largely independent cash inflows. IFRS and US GAAP require at least an annual assessment, earlier when triggering events occur. SAP has no CGU object and does not perform the impairment test; it only posts the result (course example: carrying amount EUR 48,000, recoverable EUR 30,000, impairment distributed over the CGU's assets).

Where the reports live: the area menu

The FI-AA information system is the reporting branch of the Asset Accounting area menu, which you reach with transaction ASMN. SAP List Viewer (ALV) in asset reports

All FI-AA standard reports output their results through the SAP List Viewer, which gives every report the same look and the same set of interactive functions (the ALV grid control with its toolbar icons). Why simulate?

Before year-end closing, accounting may want to know how the balance sheet would look if depreciation parameters were changed or if planned investments were realised. Simulation means an experimental change to depreciation terms (key, useful life) that is evaluated without posting anything. It can be applied to a single asset, part of the portfolio, or the entire portfolio.

Depreciation simulation for many assets: program RASIMU02

Report S_ALR_87012936 (Depreciation Forecast → Depreciation on Capitalized Assets, program RASIMU02) forecasts depreciation for a selection of assets (company code, asset class …) up to a report date that may lie several fiscal years in the future. Important options under "All Selections":

  • Evaluation period – fiscal year (annual planned depreciation) or month (monthly amounts for the current year).
  • Simulation version – substitutes alternative depreciation terms for the run.
  • Treatment of capitalizations in the current fiscal year (orders/WBS elements) – by default all values are taken as of the start of the fiscal year, so an asset capitalised during the year shows no values. Selecting "Reduce the basis for depreciation simulation of planned investments by capitalization" (or not using FY-start values) makes such assets visible. This logic avoids double counting an investment both as a planned value on an order/project and as a capitalisation on the asset (SAP Note 333818). If only active assets are simulated, the "use asset values as of FY start" setting is irrelevant and ignored.

Integration: planned capital investments can be included in the forecast. The planned amounts are managed as plan costs on an internal order or WBS element in CO/Investment Management; by assigning depreciation terms and a planned start-up date to the order or project, the program shows their future depreciation. RASIMU02 is therefore equally useful for IM colleagues running large investment measures.

Lesson 3: Understanding the Layout of an Asset History Sheet

Run the asset history sheet (program RAGITT_ALV01), understand how its rows and columns are structured, and know what can be configured in your own history sheet versions.

What the asset history sheet is

The asset history sheet is the most important FI-AA year-end report (it can also be run at interim dates). It reconciles the opening values of the fixed assets with the closing values by showing the movements of the year – acquisitions, retirements, transfers, post-capitalisations, write-ups, depreciation – separately for APC and accumulated depreciation. It supports the notes to the financial statements and is required in many legal environments, which is why SAP delivers country-specific history sheet versions that meet local statutory layouts.

How it is used

  • Like any FI-AA report it accepts a sort variant and a total level, so it can be produced as a compact totals list (group totals only) without single assets, or with every asset listed.
  • From the totals list you can drill down (double-click a group row) to the individual assets, and from an asset into the Asset Explorer.
  • Through the report-report interface (RRI) you can branch to other FI-AA reports from the history sheet.
  • Program: RAGITT_ALV01, menu Information System → Reports on Asset Accounting → Notes to Financial Statements → International → Asset History Sheet (S_ALR_87011990). Key parameters: company code, report date, sort variant, history sheet version (e.g. 0001), list assets / group totals only.

Structure and own versions

A history sheet version is a grid of history sheet items (rows = e.g. APC, accumulated depreciation; columns = e.g. opening balance, acquisitions, retirements, transfers, depreciation for the year, closing balance). When you define your own version you:

  • stay within the maximum size of 10 rows by 8 columns;
  • store the header text of every history sheet item;
  • define how each item is filled – which transaction type groups and value types feed each cell (e.g. transaction types of the acquisition group go to the "Acquisitions" column of the APC row).

Because the values are driven by transaction type groups, the history sheet is only correct if transaction types are assigned to the right groups – an important point when customers create their own transaction types.

Key takeaways
  • The asset history sheet (RAGITT_ALV01, S_ALR_87011990) reconciles opening and closing asset values via the year's movements and is the central year-end/notes report.
  • It works with any sort variant, can be shown as totals only or per asset, and allows drill-down to single assets, the Asset Explorer and other reports via the RRI.
  • History sheet versions are max. 10 × 8 items with own headers and item-fill rules; SAP delivers country-specific versions.

What the SAP List Viewer is

The SAP List Viewer (ALV) is a generic display tool: a program hands it prepared data and ALV renders a standardised, ergonomic list. Because the same tool is used throughout the system, users meet one uniform interface with the same icons everywhere. It can show simple lists and hierarchical-sequential lists (header with sub-items). Crucially, the layout can be changed after the data has been selected – no re-run of the report is needed to add a column, sort or subtotal – and the changed layout can be saved as a display variant (layout).

Generic functions

  • Select one or several columns (Ctrl-click) and move or hide them.
  • Sort ascending or descending; multi-level sorts via the Define Sort Order dialog (e.g. document type ascending, then document date descending).
  • Set and delete filters on any column (e.g. only document type SA, only amounts up to EUR 40).
  • Total a column and build subtotals per other columns (e.g. subtotal by document type or by net due date); sub-subtotals work the same way.

Working with documents from the list

FI line-item lists are not read-only. From a list you can:

  1. Select an item and choose Display Document to see the details.
  2. Choose Change Document to change it; selecting several items performs a mass change across all their documents.
  3. Choose Refresh List to see the result; if a change is not visible, a change-error log pushbutton explains why.

Whether the jump goes to the document item view or to the document overview is a checkbox in the accounting editing options (FB00).

What drilldown reporting is

Drilldown reporting is an online, interactive analysis tool built into SAP ERP Financials. SAP delivers standard drilldown reports for G/L (new G/L, table FAGLFLEXT), Profit Center Accounting, Accounts Payable and Accounts Receivable, and the tool is also used to build reports for Profitability Analysis (costing-based and account-based CO-PA) and the Special Purpose Ledger. SAP Graphics, SAP mail, printing, and Microsoft Word/Excel are connected to it. Forms for drilldown reports are created with the Report Painter.

Lesson 4: Optimizing Report Execution

Run drilldown reports in the background with selection variants and variant groups, and freeze report data in extracts so that later online calls do not hit the database.

Selection variants and variant groups

When a drilldown report is executed online, a selection screen asks for the variable values. A selection variant stores these entries together with other settings (output type, print settings – Print Report flag and list type: drilldown list, detail list or SAP List Viewer). Variants are also the basis for background execution:

  1. Create the variant on the report's selection screen (Save) with name and description; for printing in the background set Print Report and the list type (maintainable later with KE3Q in CO-PA).
  2. Define a variant group (KE3S) – a container that can hold several variants of one report (different variable combinations) and/or variants of different reports.
  3. Maintain the group (KE3P): assign report + variant pairs (per operating concern and type of profitability analysis).
  4. Schedule the group (KE3R): a background job is created; set the start condition (immediate or periodic) and release it.
  5. Monitor in SM37 and read the spool output.

Variant groups therefore let you schedule many report runs as one job at times of low system load, optionally at regular intervals.

Report data extracts (frozen data)

When a report is executed – online or in the background – its result can be saved as an extract (Report → Save data, or confirming the prompt on exit). The next time the report is run with exactly the same selection parameters, the system asks whether to read the saved data or generate a new report; with different parameters no prompt appears because no matching extract exists. Reading an extract avoids reading transaction data from the database, so runtime drops dramatically and the database server is spared – SAP recommends frozen data for complex, high-volume reports. Technically the data is held in buffer table COIX_DATA. The trade-off: the user sees data as of the extract time, not live data.

Key takeaways
  • Selection variants store variable values, output type and print settings of a drilldown report.
  • Variant groups bundle variants of one or several reports and are scheduled as one background job (KE3S/KE3P/KE3R in CO-PA, SM37 for monitoring).
  • Extracts freeze report data; a rerun with identical parameters offers the saved data and needs no database access.
  • Use frozen data for complex reports with large data volumes.

Lesson 6: Using Variables with Drilldown Reports

Distinguish global from local variables, use offsets to derive values such as the previous year from one entry, and use text variables so that column headings follow the selected values.

Global vs. local variables

Variables make a report reusable: the same definition produces different results depending on the values entered at run time.

TypeWhere definedScopeNotes
Global variableCustomizing (variable maintenance per application class)All forms and reportsUse for frequently needed variables such as fiscal year (1FY) and period; changing it affects every form/report using it. SAP-delivered variables start with a digit
Local variableIn the form (applies to all reports using the form) or in the report (applies to that report only)The form/reportName up to eight characters; in texts referenced with a leading $ (e.g. $FY)

A variable is identified by its name and its use (characteristic value, text, formula …), and characteristic-value variables additionally by the field they refer to. Each variable has a replacement type: manual (user enters the value, possibly obligatory) or automatic (system derives it).

Offsets: previous year from one entry

Numeric characteristic-value variables can be linked to constants with an operator and offset. Example form: column 1 = fiscal year with variable 1FY (or local FY); column 2 = same variable with operator "-" and offset 1; column 3 = offset 2. At execution the system asks for the fiscal year only once and computes the other columns automatically. In the element definition this is done by ticking Variable on/off for the characteristic, selecting the variable and entering operator/offset (Change variable entry).

Text variables

Column or row headings can be variable too. In the element texts a global text variable is entered as &1FY, a local one as $FY. Text variables exist only in forms, are replaced when the report is executed, and global ones must exist in Customizing first. For a local text variable you define (Extras → Variables → Variable definition) the replacement: usually automatic from the From-field of a characteristic (e.g. RYEAR fiscal year, offset 1, length 4), so the heading shows the actual year selected. Automatic replacement works only if the variable refers to a characteristic that is used in the element. The text length is fixed in the variable definition; for a manually entered replacement it equals the variable name length.

Key takeaways
  • Global variables (Customizing, e.g. 1FY) are shared by all forms and reports; local variables belong to one form or report.
  • Operator/offset on a variable (1FY − 1) derives related values from a single user entry.
  • Text variables (&global, $local) make headings dynamic; local text variables are usually replaced automatically from a characteristic's From-field.
  • Replacement type decides whether the user enters the value or the system derives it.

Lesson 7: Exporting Data

Export drilldown report data to a spreadsheet in XXL format or to Microsoft Word, and know when the export function is available.

The Export button writes the report in XXL format, a special format for transferring list data to spreadsheet applications. All selected characteristics of the report can be transferred, so the breakdown can be continued in the spreadsheet (e.g. as a pivot table). Conditions for the export:

  • The current list must be a drilldown list – for technical reasons it is not available from a detail list.
  • Values must be displayed as absolute values; when the list is switched to percentages the function is inactive.

Procedure: Report → Export → Copy report to XXL → choose the characteristics (All characteristics is recommended; selecting only some can produce a list too large for the spreadsheet) → choose Pivot table or table → choose Microsoft Excel → save the file locally. The Word interface downloads drilldown reports to Microsoft Word for printing; this works for all reports except those called from another report via the report-to-report interface.

Key takeaways
  • Export uses the XXL format to hand the full characteristic breakdown to Excel (table or pivot table).
  • Only possible from a drilldown list with absolute values, not from detail lists or percentage views.
  • Word download allows formatted printing, except for reports reached via the report-to-report interface.

Lesson 8: Adding Calculations to Drilldown Reports

Define individual cells in a two-axis form and use them in formulas to calculate figures – such as the year-on-year change in fixed assets – that cannot be derived from a row/column intersection alone.

Cell definition

Normally a cell's content follows from the intersection of its row and column definitions. When that is not enough, you define the cell explicitly: place the cursor on the cell and choose Edit → Element → Define element (or double-click). The cell types are:

Cell typeMeaning
SelectedThe cell keeps its intersection value but becomes available as an operand (Z00n) in formulas
InactiveThe cell is switched off; it is empty and shows 0 in the form (e.g. the ratio cells in a column where they make no sense)
FormulaOffered once at least one cell is selected; the formula editor opens and the cell is computed from other cells; it may carry its own cell text and need not equal the row/column intersection

Example: change in fixed assets

In an asset form with one column per fiscal year, the fixed-asset value of each year is a selected cell (Z001 current year, Z002 previous year, Z003, Z004). A row "Change to fixed assets" then holds formula cells Z001 − Z002, Z002 − Z003, Z003 − Z004; the last column has no successor and its cell is set inactive. To separate increases from decreases, the functions MX0 (maximum of operand and zero) and MN0 (minimum of operand and zero) are used: increase = MX0(Z001 − Z002), decrease = MN0(Z001 − Z002), and so on.

Key takeaways
  • Cells are defined only when row × column does not determine the content; types are Selected, Inactive, Formula.
  • Selected cells become operands Z00n for cell formulas; inactive cells show 0.
  • MX0/MN0 split a movement into increases and decreases – the pattern used for asset-movement rows.

Lesson 9: Using Key Figure Schemes and Characteristic Hierarchies in Profitability Analysis Reporting

Use key figure schemes to give calculated CO-PA figures (net revenue, contribution margins, operating profit) one consistent definition, and characteristic hierarchies to group characteristic values for reporting and what-if analysis.

Key figure scheme

A key figure scheme is a set of formulas, each called an element, that defines key figures for costing-based CO-PA. Elements are built from value fields, constants and earlier elements of the same scheme using arithmetic operators or user-defined ABAP functions in the formula editor; each element has texts and display parameters. Once defined, the elements can be used in forms and reports, so "Contribution Margin I" means the same in every report – which is exactly the consistency a CEO asks for. The function Check key figure scheme verifies syntax, parentheses, empty formulas, recursive formulas and circular references. The course uses the delivered scheme "Sales Control" (CO-PA Customizing → Report Components) as an example.

Characteristic hierarchies

In CO-PA you can arrange the values of a characteristic (products, customers …) hierarchically and use the hierarchy in drilldown reports; several variants of the same hierarchy allow simulations and what-if analyses. Rules:

  • A hierarchy is built from the master data (check table) of the characteristic; characteristics without a check/text table cannot have hierarchies.
  • Characteristics sharing one master data table share the same hierarchy.
  • A hierarchy contains values of one characteristic only, and each value may occur only once in it.

Account-based vs. costing-based CO-PA (exercise insights)

  • In account-based CO-PA of operating concern IDEA, Customer and Product are not available as report characteristics because they are not object-level characteristics there – no separate totals records exist for their values.
  • The record type (F = billing, etc.) marks the source of data in costing-based CO-PA; account-based CO-PA stores its data in the general CO transaction tables and therefore has no record type.
  • In account-based basic reports put Cost Element first in the sort order – amounts in the columns only make sense in connection with a cost element.
  • Key figures in account-based CO-PA are the fixed basic key figures, e.g. Value in object currency, Value in transaction currency, Value in controlling area currency.
Key takeaways
  • Key figure schemes centralise formula definitions (elements) for CO-PA reporting and can be syntax-checked.
  • Characteristic hierarchies group the values of one characteristic (from master data), each value once; variants support what-if analysis.
  • Account-based CO-PA lacks record type and some object-level characteristics; sort cost element first.

Lesson 10: Connecting Drilldown Reports

Link reports through the report-to-report interface (RRI) so that users can jump from a summarised drilldown report to more detailed receiver reports or transactions.

A single report covering many characteristics or changing characteristic combinations would be too large to run online. The RRI solves this by chaining several reports, each with few characteristics: from a selected cell, row, column or block the user calls a receiver report, and the current selection is passed on. Receiver objects can be other drilldown reports, report portfolios (EIS), queries, ABAP reports, Report Writer reports and transactions – including reports of other application classes, so an FI balance report can call the G/L line-item report. In the report definition (FGI2 change report) choose the Options tab → Report assignment, insert a row, pick the report type (drilldown report, or Other report type → e.g. RT ABAP report program) and the receiver, e.g. program FAGL_ACCOUNT_ITEMS_GL (G/L line items, new G/L). At run time the user selects a value and chooses Call up report; if several receivers exist a list is offered.

Key takeaways
  • The RRI chains small, fast reports instead of one huge report; selections are passed to the receiver.
  • Receivers: drilldown reports, report portfolios, queries, ABAP reports, Report Writer reports, transactions – across application classes.
  • Assignment is made in the report definition (Options → Report assignment); invoked with Call up report.
